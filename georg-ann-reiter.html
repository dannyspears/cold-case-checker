The Georg Ann Reiter case: two women, four rifle shots, and a story detectives no longer fully accept.

Late on Sept 8 or in the early hours of Sept 9, 1970, Georg Ann Reiter, 21, and a friend stopped at a park on Ohio Route 128 in Butler County. Both were shot with a .22 caliber rifle. Reiter died; her friend survived. The survivor says a man in a suit did it. Detectives have said that is unlikely.

Cold Case Checker's conclusion · Oct 2026

Case file at a glance

This is a case where the central witness and the detectives disagree. The survivor has long said a man with a rifle demanded the car. Butler County detectives have publicly doubted that, offering a love-triangle theory and also saying a random shooter is possible. Nobody has been charged.

What happened

Dates and places come from the Ohio Attorney General and two WCPO reports. Outlets disagree on some points, which are noted.

Late Sept 8 – early Sept 9, 1970

The park

Georg Ann Reiter, 21, of Hamilton, and a friend stop at a park along State Route 128 in Butler County. Local outlets name the place as St. Clair Township (Attorney General), Fairfield Township, or Ross Township; the park no longer exists. The Attorney General dates the shooting Sept 9, while WCPO places it late on Sept 8 and says Reiter was pronounced dead the next morning.

That night

The shooting

The survivor has told police several times, including in a 1970 audio statement, that a man knocked on the car window with a rifle and wanted the car. She dropped the keys, and the women were marched into the woods. Four rounds were fired from a .22 caliber rifle. Both women were hit.

Afterward

Help arrives

The survivor finds the keys, drives to the road, and flags traffic. A semi-truck driver helps get Reiter taken for help. She dies, shot in the back, according to her brother.

1970

Sketch and statement

The survivor works with a sketch artist. An independent witness driving home from a shift at Fernald gives a handwritten statement that he saw a man in a dress coat pacing near a car. He has since died.

2003 – 2004

Old investigators

An earlier WCPO report says a retired detective, Frank Smith, matched a man already in prison for another murder to a 1970 photo in 2003; the man neither admitted nor denied it. A 2004 interview of the survivor in Florida is described by current detectives as evasive.

2017

A new review

Butler County Sheriff's Det. Dan Turner and Lt. Ed Tanner begin reviewing the case after a call from Reiter's brother. Detectives later contact the Ohio Bureau of Criminal Investigation for help.

About 2019

WCPO I-Team report

Detectives tell WCPO they believe a jealousy motive in a love triangle. The report quotes them saying the shooter may have been a woman, probably in her 70s if alive, and likely in Butler County, and that they reject the account of a man in a business suit. This is a theory by detectives, not a finding.

2022 – 2023

Anniversary reports

WCPO and FOX19 report that detectives say a love triangle is possible but a random shooter is also possible. Detectives traveled to Florida in spring 2022 to speak with the survivor but were told she was out of town. The family hopes to see evidence, some of which they believe has been lost.

Victims

A softball player and musician, described by those who knew her as a great person with no enemies.

Died

Georg Ann Reiter, 21

Born Nov 8, 1948 (per the Attorney General), of Hamilton, Ohio. Described as a tomboy, a softball player, and a talented musician. Her parents and her twin sister, Geo Ann, are deceased. Her brother, George, was home from military service a month before her death. Some outlets give her age as 22.

Survived

The survivor

Reiter's friend was also shot and was hospitalized for days. She has declined most comment since the 1970s and lives out of state. Local reports name her; we do not, because she is a private person and a survivor.

What would settle it

  3. Locate the lost evidence that the family believes was misplaced.
Cold Case Checker's assessment

Two stories, one answer

We put the chance that the attack was random roughly 40% and the chance that it was personal at roughly 45%. The rest is uncertain. Detectives' own statements have shifted, which tells us the record cannot yet separate them. We put the chance of a resolution in five years at 5%.

Our hypothesis

Editorial reading of public records. A working theory, not a finding, and it names no individual.

Working theory · confidence stated per point

A shooting at a lonely park that the surviving evidence cannot yet explain

  1. Both women were shot. Four rounds from a .22 rifle at two people suggests an intent to kill both, not a robbery that went wrong.
  2. The car was either the motive or a cover. A demand for the car fits a robbery. Detectives doubt it.
  3. The detectives' shift is telling. They have said a personal motive is possible and a random shooter is also possible.
  4. Everything depends on a record that may be lost. The 1970 audio statement, the sketch, and ballistics are the keys.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
