The Chaz Dudley case: a passenger in the wrong car at 4 a.m., and a mother who turned her grief into a movement.

At about 4:10 a.m. on Sept 29, 2007, Daniel "Chaz" Dudley, 26, was riding as a passenger with friends near Galbraith Road and Vine Street in Cincinnati when another car pulled up and fired into the car he was in. Two people were shot. Dudley died at University Hospital. Police believe he was not the intended target. No one has been charged.

Case file at a glance

His mother, Hope Dudley, founded the nonprofit UCanSpeakForMe, which puts unsolved-homicide posters and cold-case playing cards in Ohio prisons to draw out tips; Chaz is on the five of hearts. Nineteen years later she still says, "I know that justice is coming." The Ohio Attorney General lists the case as unsolved homicide 1051.

What happened

Details come from the Ohio Attorney General (case 1051), WKRC (Sept 2026), and Hoodline (Sept 2026), which relays WCPO. Sources describe the location slightly differently.

Sept 29, 2007 · about 4:10 a.m.

The shooting

Dudley, 26, is riding as a passenger with friends. Investigators say the suspects' car drove past as his car turned from Vine Street onto Galbraith Road near Hartwell School and fired multiple shots into it. The Attorney General places the shooting at 29 West Galbraith Road. His Crime Stoppers cold-case card, posted on the Attorney General listing, says he had just left the Ritz Night Club at that address in Hartwell.

Sept 29, 2007

Found at a gas station

Cincinnati District 4 officers respond, then are redirected by Hamilton County sheriff's deputies to the Speedway station at Galbraith and Pippin roads, where two shooting victims are found.

Sept 29, 2007

He dies

Colerain medics take both victims to University Hospital, where Dudley dies.

  3. Re-interview the friends in the car and the second victim, with fresh questions.
Cold Case Checker's assessment

A targeted drive-by aimed at someone else in or near the car

We put the chance that the shooters were after another person in the car at roughly 70%, as police believe. We put the chance of a charge within five years at roughly 8%, because the case depends on witnesses coming forward.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
