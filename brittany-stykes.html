The Brittany Stykes case: an ambush on a country road, and a toddler who survived.

On Aug 28, 2013, Brittany Stykes, 22 and pregnant, was shot to death in her Jeep on Ohio 68 near Georgetown while driving to her father's birthday celebration. Her toddler daughter was shot in the head and survived. No one has been charged.

Case file at a glance

This is one of the best-resourced unsolved cases in the region: more than 75 interviews, about $1 million spent, and a $50,000 reward. It still has no named suspect, no motive, and no shell casings. The family says it is no closer to an answer, and the sheriff says the case is active.

What happened

Details come from FOX19 reports in 2023 and 2026. Times of day are not in the reports we read.

Aug 28, 2013

The drive

Brittany Stykes, 22 and pregnant, drives her yellow Jeep on Ohio 68 near Georgetown toward her father's birthday celebration. Her toddler daughter is with her. Security camera footage shows the Jeep shortly before the shooting.

Aug 28, 2013

The shooting

She is shot multiple times. Her daughter is shot in the head and survives. The Jeep is found deep in the woods with Stykes dead at the wheel. No shell casings are found.

2013 to 2018

Her husband at the time

Detectives first focus on her husband at the time. After interviews and polygraphs, the sheriff's office says he is no longer considered a suspect. He has denied involvement.

2017 to 2018

The Jeep

Her husband sues the sheriff's office for the Jeep and wins. It is returned to him in 2018.

Aug 28, 2023

Ten years

The sheriff's office says it has done more than 75 interviews and spent about $1 million. A $50,000 reward is offered. Detectives say they are using new technology to develop leads.

Mar 31, 2026

The family asks for a state review

Her father says the family has asked the sheriff and prosecutor to request a review by the Ohio Attorney General's cold-case unit and has had no luck so far.

Aug 2026

Thirteen years

At a memorial on Ohio 68, Sheriff Christopher Hodges says the office is still working the case and will follow up on any leads. No new leads are reported.

What would settle it

  1. An independent review of the file by the state cold-case unit or another agency.
  2. Identify the vehicle or vehicles on the road that day from the security footage and other records.
  3. Re-test the Jeep and any retained items with current methods.
Cold Case Checker's assessment

A planned attack, probably by someone who knew her route

We put the chance that the attack was targeted rather than random at roughly 75%, because it was an ambush on a known drive with no robbery reported. We put the chance of a solution within five years at roughly 12%, because the physical evidence appears thin.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
