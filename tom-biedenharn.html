The Tom Biedenharn case: a beloved Lawrenceburg volunteer found dead on Memorial Day, and a bright blue truck in the neighborhood.

On Memorial Day, May 28, 2018, Tom Biedenharn, 73, was found dead on Highridge Court in the Hidden Valley subdivision near Lawrenceburg, Indiana. An autopsy ruled his death a homicide. Indiana State Police believe more than one person was with him. No one has been charged.

Case file at a glance

Biedenharn was a former Elder High School and University of Cincinnati basketball player who helped develop Lauren Hill Memorial Park and volunteered with people in recovery. State police want to identify a newer-model "electric or bright blue" four-door or extended-cab truck seen in the area. His family offered a $50,000 reward in 2018.

Cold Case Checker's assessment

A planned or opportunistic attack by two or more people he may have known

We put the chance that the killers knew him or knew his routine at roughly 60%. We put the chance of an arrest within five years at roughly 12%, because of the reward and the truck.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
