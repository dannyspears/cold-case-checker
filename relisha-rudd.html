Case file · Relisha Rudd Case
Updated November 15, 2026Status: missing, unsolved
STATUSRelisha has never been found, and her death has not been confirmed. The FBI offers up to $25,000 for information leading to her recovery. Call D.C. police at 202-727-9099 or text 50411, or the FBI at 1-800-CALL-FBI.

Relisha Rudd: an 8-year-old from a city homeless shelter was out of school for weeks before anyone reported her missing. The man she was last seen with is dead, and she has never been found.

Relisha Rudd, 8, lived with her family at the D.C. General family shelter in Washington. Kahlil Tatum, a 51-year-old janitor at the shelter, had befriended her family and was allowed to take her out. She stopped coming to school in February 2014; her school was told she was sick and in the care of a "Dr. Tatum." Hotel cameras recorded her with Tatum on February 26 and March 1, 2014, the last confirmed sighting. A school counselor finally alerted authorities, and police opened a missing-person case on March 19. The next day Tatum's wife, Andrea, was found shot dead in a motel in Oxon Hill, Maryland. On March 31 Tatum was found dead in Kenilworth Park, an apparent suicide. Investigators believe he killed Relisha, but her body has never been found, and the case remains open.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
