Christopher Walker II case: three days after his 30th birthday, he was shot in a robbery at dinnertime in one of Houston's busiest shopping districts. No one has been charged.

Christopher Walker II, 30, was shot in a parking lot in the 5000 block of Westheimer Road, near Post Oak Boulevard in Houston's Galleria area, at about 6:10 p.m. on Thursday, January 16, 2025. He had just left a restaurant, and police believe he was robbed; his wallet and belongings were taken. A friend drove him in a pickup truck to an emergency room on San Felipe Street, where he died. Police described the gunman as a Black man in his 20s in a green hoodie, and Crime Stoppers says the shooter fled in a light-colored four-door sedan. He had turned 30 three days earlier. No one has been charged.

What happened

Details come from ABC13 (Jan 17, 2025), FOX 26 (Jan 16-17, 2025), KPRC (Jul 22, 2025), the Crime Stoppers of Houston listing and flyer, and his obituary. Early reports differ on the time and some details; we show each version.

Jan 13, 1995

Born

His obituary gives his birth date as January 13, 1995 (McDuffie Mortuary).

Jan 13, 2025

Turns 30

His father told KPRC he had just celebrated his 30th birthday (KPRC, Jul 2025).

Jan 16, 2025 · about 6:10 p.m.

Shot in a parking lot

After leaving a restaurant, he is shot in the parking lot of a business in the 5000 block of Westheimer, near Post Oak. People call 911 about gunshots (ABC13; Crime Stoppers). Police later say it did not happen outside Morton's The Steakhouse, as first reported, but at another business nearby (FOX 26).

Jan 16 · minutes later

Emergency room

A friend drives him in a pickup truck (silver per ABC13, gray per FOX 26) to Post Oak Emergency Room, 5018 San Felipe, and carries him inside. Staff try to save him; he is pronounced dead. KPRC says he died within eight minutes of arrival (ABC13; FOX 26; KPRC). ABC13's first report put the arrival at about 5:30 p.m.

Jan 17, 2025

Named; suspect described

HPD names him and describes the suspect as a Black man in his 20s in a green hoodie; motive believed to be robbery (ABC13).

Jan 24, 2025

Celebration of life

A memorial service is held at The Fountain of Praise in Houston (McDuffie Mortuary).

Jul 2025

Reward doubled

Crime Stoppers lists the case (up to $5,000; the shooter fled in a light-colored four-door sedan). On July 22 KPRC reports the reward doubled to $10,000, and his father speaks publicly for the first time (Crime Stoppers; KPRC).

Cold Case Checker's assessment

A robbery by someone who did not know him

We put the chance that this was a robbery by a stranger or strangers, as police say, at roughly 80%: his belongings were taken in a busy commercial lot, and police have described a robbery. That is our judgment, not a finding. We put the chance of a charge within five years at roughly 35%: the area is full of cameras and there is a suspect description and a getaway car, but no arrest after 21 months.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
