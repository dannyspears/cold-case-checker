Chase Isles case: a father shot in the stairwell of a building where he did not live, and a neighbor who heard him say he had a daughter.

At about 7:22 p.m. on Monday, November 16, 2020, Jackson Township police answered a report of gunshots at an apartment building in the 4400 block of South Boulevard NW, near Canton, Ohio. They found Charles "Chase" Isles III, 28, dead just inside the building, in a stairwell. He had been shot multiple times. He did not live there, and his family believes he went to meet a friend. No one has been charged.

Case file at a glance

A neighbor told News 5 Cleveland she heard him say, "You really going to shoot me? I have a daughter." His brother, the U.S. Olympic rugby sevens player Carlin Isles, has used his large social media following to ask for tips. Police have said little in public: in 2020 the chief said detectives were following leads, and in 2025 the department asked for more witnesses.

Cold Case Checker's assessment

Someone who knew he was coming to the building that evening

We put the chance that the gunman knew Chase or knew he would be there at roughly 70%, because he was killed inside a building he was visiting, not on a street, and his family believes he went to meet someone. That is our judgment, not a finding. We put the chance of a charge within five years at roughly 12%: the case is recent, there were witnesses nearby, and police are still asking for help.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
