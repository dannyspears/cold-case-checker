Nickeisha Holloman case: she got into a white or silver Hyundai on July 1, 2018. Seven weeks later she was found under mulch in Mount Airy.

On July 1, 2018, Nickeisha "Keisha" Holloman, 23, walked out of the Crossroads Center at 3009 Burnet Avenue in Cincinnati and got into a white or silver Hyundai Sonata, according to police. She was on her way to get her hair braided, her mother said, and never arrived. On August 22, 2018, skeletal remains were found in an empty lot in the 1800 block of Mehmert Avenue in Mount Airy. In October the coroner identified them as hers and ruled her death a homicide.

Case file at a glance

Police released the video of the car in January 2019 and asked one question: who was driving it? The lead detective added a second: her body was under a heavy layer of mulch, so did someone buy mulch around July 1 and act strangely? Neither question has been answered in public. A $2,500 Crime Stoppers reward is offered.

What happened

Details come from the Ohio Attorney General listing (case 3142), WCPO, WKRC, 700WLW and FOX19 reports from July 2018 to January 2019. A 2022 true-crime blog summary adds nothing police did not say. We found no news after 2019.

Spring 2018

At the Crossroads Center

Nickeisha is staying at the Crossroads Center on Burnet Avenue for a 45-day, court-ordered behavioral-health stay after a run-in with police (FOX19, Aug 2018, from her mother). She has type 2 diabetes and needs insulin (WCPO; FOX19).

July 1, 2018

She leaves

She leaves the center. Her mother says she was going to get her hair braided and never made the appointment, and that she called that day to give her a new phone number (FOX19). FOX19 reported she left with her boyfriend; her mother says he told her they argued and she left with someone else. Police have not named him or said whether he is a suspect (FOX19, Oct 2018).

July 1, 2018

The car

Surveillance video shows her walking to and getting into a white or silver Hyundai Sonata (police, via FOX19, Jan 2019).

July 6-7, 2018

Critical missing alert

Police issue a critical missing alert: she is 5'3", 143 pounds, and without her medication, and may be in a vehicle headed toward Florida (WKRC; WCPO; 700WLW). People at the center said she had talked about Florida (FOX19).

July 30 - Aug 1, 2018

A mother's plea

Police renew the critical missing alert. Her mother, Keiana Rogers, tells FOX19: "Something happened to her." Her phone is off and her social media is silent.

Aug 22, 2018

Remains found

Officers are called to an empty lot in the 1800 block of Mehmert Avenue for skeletal remains (FOX19; WCPO). A forensic anthropologist examines them.

Oct 9, 2018

Identified

Police and the Hamilton County Coroner identify the remains as Nickeisha Holloman and rule her death a homicide. Police release no suspect information (WCPO; FOX19; WKRC; 700WLW).

Jan 15, 2019

The video

Police release the video of her getting into the Hyundai. Det. David Gregory: "We're really trying to find where that vehicle is and who was driving it at the time." He asks about mulch purchases around July 1. A $2,500 reward is offered (FOX19).

Aug 2024

Cold case task force

Cincinnati police and Hamilton County launch a cold case task force. We do not know whether it has reviewed this case.

Nov 2026

Our check

The state still lists the case as unsolved. We found no arrest, charge or news report after 2019.

Our hypothesis

Editorial reading of public records. A working theory, not a finding, and it names no individual.

Working theory · confidence stated per point

Someone she rode with, or met soon after, killed her and hid her in Mount Airy

  1. The car is the thread. The Hyundai is the last confirmed sighting. Whoever drove it either harmed her or can say where she went next.
  2. Mulch means planning. Covering a body with a heavy layer of mulch takes time, tools and a vehicle. That points to someone who had both and knew the lot.
  3. She meant to come back. She gave her mother a new number and planned to help her grandfather. That argues against a voluntary trip to Florida.
  4. Seven weeks hid the cause. Skeletal remains may not show how she died, which may be why police released none.
  5. A fresh look is possible. Vehicle records, phone data from 2018 and the city's 2024 cold case task force could still identify the driver.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
