Updated October 27, 2026Status: unsolved
STATUSOne man was charged in 2016; two juries could not agree (2017, 2018) and no third trial has been held. No one has been convicted. Call the Panola County Sheriff at 662-563-6230 or the FBI at 601-948-5000; Mississippi Crime Stoppers 1-800-729-2169 is anonymous.

Jessica Chambers case: a 19-year-old walked out of a burning car on a country road. Two juries could not agree on who set the fire.

Jessica Chambers, 19, was found a little after 8 p.m. on Saturday, December 6, 2014, walking away from her burning car on Herron Road near Courtland, in Panola County, Mississippi. She was burned over more than 90% of her body and died hours later at a Memphis hospital. First responders said she told them "Eric" did it; investigators ruled out every Eric and Derek they found. In 2016 a Courtland man, Quinton Tellis, was charged with capital murder. His first trial in 2017 and his second in 2018 both ended in mistrials, and we found no third trial. No one has been convicted.

Case file at a glance

Small town, a Saturday evening, a car on fire on a country road. Someone in Courtland knows who was with Jessica in the 90 minutes before she was found. The FBI once offered $25,000 for that answer.

What happened

Details come from the FBI (Jan 2015), CBS News and the Associated Press (2014-2017), Fox News (Jan 2015), FOX13 Memphis, Oxygen and WLBT (2018), the Clarion Ledger (2024) and Action News 5 (2026). Some details, such as the exact Herron Road address, differ by outlet.

Dec 6, 2014 · about 6:48 p.m.

Last call home

She tells her mother by phone she is going to get something to eat (CBS/AP, Dec 2014).

Dec 6 · evening

Last seen on video

Store video near her home shows her at a convenience store, the last confirmed sighting. Investigators said about 90 minutes were unaccounted for (CBS/AP, Dec 10, 2014).

Dec 6 · a little after 8 p.m.

Found on Herron Road

Firefighters responding to a car fire find her walking away from her burning car, burned over more than 90% of her body (CBS/WREG; CBS/AP). Some reports say 98%.

Hours later

Death

She dies at a Memphis hospital. The coroner rules thermal injuries the cause (FOX13 Memphis).

Jan 2015

FBI reward

The FBI offers up to $25,000 for information (FBI Jackson, Jan 2015; Fox News, Jan 18, 2015).

Feb 24, 2016

Indictment

A grand jury indicts Quinton Tellis of Courtland for capital murder and arson. The district attorney says he acted alone (CBS News, Feb 24, 2016).

Oct 2017

First trial

Prosecutors say cell phone records put the two together until about 7:30 p.m., and that he lied about when he last saw her. The defense says she named "Eric," not him (CBS/AP).

Oct 16, 2017

First mistrial

The jury cannot reach a unanimous verdict; reports say seven of twelve voted guilty (CBS/AP).

Oct 1, 2018

Second mistrial

A second jury deadlocks; the defense says the split was 6-6 (WLBT; Oxygen).

2018 onward

No third trial

The district attorney did not commit to a third trial. We found no third trial and no dismissal reported (Clarion Ledger via Yahoo, Oct 2024).

Jul 23, 2026

Louisiana verdict

A jury in Louisiana finds Tellis not guilty in the 2015 killing of a university student there, a separate case (Action News 5, Jul 24, 2026).

Oct 2026

Still unresolved

No one has been convicted of killing Jessica Chambers.

Cold Case Checker's assessment

Someone she knew set the fire

We put the chance that the person who set the fire knew her, rather than being a stranger, at roughly 80%: she was in her own car in her home area, and the case against the only man charged rests on time spent with her that evening. That is our judgment, not a finding. We put the chance of a conviction within five years at roughly 15%: two juries could not agree, no new evidence has been announced, and no third trial has been scheduled.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
