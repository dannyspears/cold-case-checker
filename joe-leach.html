Case file · Joe Leach Case
Updated November 1, 2026Status: open, unsolved
STATUSThe Ohio Attorney General lists the case as unsolved homicide 2026 (North College Hill Police). The gunman is on surveillance video in a red hoodie and has never been identified. A $5,000 reward has been offered for years. Call Crime Stoppers at 513-352-3040 (anonymous).

Joe Leach case: a masked robber in a red hoodie shot "Papa Joe" in the back in his own garage. Cameras caught him coming and going.

At about 1:50 p.m. on January 5, 2016, a man with a gun walked into Leach Brothers Automotive, 1949 W. Galbraith Road, at the corner of Simpson Avenue in North College Hill, and demanded money. The owner, Larry "Joe" Leach, 64, tried to get away and was shot once in the back. He told officers his attacker was a Black man in a red hoodie. He died at University of Cincinnati Medical Center on January 8.

Case file at a glance

Police released surveillance video the same day: a figure crossing Simpson Avenue, entering the shop and running off. A police dog lost the trail in the alley behind the business. Police said they believed the gunman was local and had been around the shop before. Years later, his daughter says, police found the murder weapon, but could not tie it to the person who fired it.

Before 2016

Papa Joe

Joe Leach runs Leach Brothers Automotive on Galbraith Road and is a lifelong bracket racer, known for helping anyone at the track and fixing cars on IOUs for customers down on their luck (Dragzine). Later in life he went back to Cincinnati State and earned a degree (FOX19, 2025).

"Help me!"

An employee working under the hood of a truck hears Joe come into the garage and yell "Help me!", then a gunshot, and finds him on the floor. He never sees the gunman (AG).

Joe, conscious, describes the gunman as a Black man in a red hoodie. Police dogs track him to the alley behind the business and lose the trail (AG). Police post surveillance video that day (WCPO).

Our hypothesis

Editorial reading of public records. A working theory, not a finding, and it names no individual.

Working theory · confidence stated per point

A local robber who knew the shop, panicked, and fired

  1. He knew where to go. Police said he had likely been around the shop before. He came on foot and left down the alley.
  2. It was meant to be a robbery. He demanded money; he fired when Joe ran.
  3. He covered his face. He expected to be recognized, which again suggests a local.
  4. The gun moved. If the murder weapon turned up later with someone else, it was passed on or sold; its chain of owners leads back toward him.
  5. Someone knows. Robberies are rarely kept secret for long. A $5,000 reward is waiting.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
