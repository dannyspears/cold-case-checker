The Dumler family murders: three adults bound and shot, two children spared, a scene wiped clean.

In October 1969, Martin and Patricia Dumler and Patricia's mother, Mary Wilson, were found shot to death in their Mount Lookout home in Cincinnati. There was no forced entry and no sign of robbery. The only fingerprints belonged to the victims. The case is open.

Case file at a glance

The Dumler case looks personal and professional at once: no robbery, no forced entry, a wiped scene, and children left alive. That points to someone who knew the family or planned carefully, but after 57 years police still say they cannot point in one direction.

What happened

Dates come from a FOX19 report. Items marked unverified come from author J.T. Townsend and are disputed by the family.

Oct 22–23, 1969 · Beverly Hill Drive

The killings

Martin and Patricia Dumler and Mary Wilson, Patricia's mother, are killed in the family home in Mount Lookout. The killer forces all three into an upstairs bedroom and binds them with household cords (lamp, TV, and appliance cords).

That night

The shooting

Records show all three were shot to death. The killer removed the cords from their arms and ankles and left. Per Townsend (unverified), he returned up to an hour later and stabbed the couple in the chest, and pillows were used to muffle the gunshots.

Next morning

The children

The couple's children, ages 5 and 4, who were downstairs and unharmed, go to a neighbor's door and say they cannot wake their parents.

1969

The investigation

Police give about 20 polygraphs, interview at least 100 people, and check hundreds of tips. They write to other agencies, including Atlanta and Los Angeles, asking about the Manson murders and similar cases. Nothing links.

1969 and after

Bricca question

Police meet with the Hamilton County Sheriff's Office about a possible link to the 1966 Bricca family murders, and rule it out.

2021

52 years later

Police say they have no suspect and need more information. A family statement says a tip was sent to Crime Stoppers about 15 years earlier and was worked with homicide detectives (not confirmed by police).

Victims

Three adults and two children who became orphans.

  3. Ballistics. Compare the bullets or casings with any weapon later recovered in the area.
Cold Case Checker's assessment

A planned crime by someone who knew the house

We put the chance that the killer knew the family or the household at roughly 70%, because there was no forced entry and the children were spared. We put the chance that more than one person was involved at roughly 45%, since three adults were controlled and bound. Neither can be tested without retained evidence.

Our hypothesis

Editorial reading of public records. A working theory, not a finding, and it names no individual.

Working theory · confidence stated per point

Targeted, planned, and cleaned up, by someone who either knew the household or had a reason to be there

  1. This was not a robbery. Nothing was taken that police could identify, and the house was not ransacked.
  2. The killer controlled three adults. Binding and moving three people suggests a weapon, planning, or more than one person.
  3. The cleanup was deliberate. A wiped scene suggests someone who thought about evidence.
  4. The best path is the family and workplace record. A fresh look at Martin Dumler's business and personal contacts is not public.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
