The Stephenson murders: a church deacon, a staged scene, and a fingerprint that does not fit the DNA.

On May 29, 2011, Bill and Peggy Stephenson, both 74, were found dead in their Florence, Kentucky condo after they missed church. Both were stabbed and beaten. The scene had been staged. Bill ran a ministry for truckers at a nearby truck stop. The case is open.

Cold Case Checker's conclusion · Oct 2026

Case file at a glance

The Stephenson case has the rare thing cold cases usually lack: a DNA profile from one person and a partial fingerprint. They do not point to the same man, and the question is why. The detective on the case calls it "not cold, just old."

VictimsBill and Peggy, 74
DateMay 29, 2011
PlaceFlorence, Kentucky
CauseStabbing, blunt force
Reward$50,000 (2020)
Lead agencyBoone County Sheriff
  1. A staged scene.The detective says every room had something staged. The scene looked like a burglary, but valuables were left behind. A ritual look was concluded to be staged.
  2. A killer's DNA, unmatched.In Aug 2012 the Kentucky State Police lab developed a DNA profile from one person found in several places. It is in CODIS with no match.
  3. A fingerprint match with no DNA match.A partial fingerprint sent to labs in all 50 states was reportedly matched in 2023. The man's DNA did not match the scene.
  4. A truck-stop ministry.Bill ran Truckers Chapel at a Florence truck stop. A man was seen two days before banging on the chapel door and asking for him.

What happened

Dates come from a 2020 Spectrum News report, the Crime Junkie case page, and the Boone County Sheriff's Office. The 2023 fingerprint item is from a podcast page and is unconfirmed.

May 27, 2011

A man at the chapel

Witnesses see an unidentified man banging on the door of the Truckers Chapel at the Florence truck stop, asking for Bill Stephenson.

May 28, 2011

The last sighting

The couple is last seen alive at a granddaughter's graduation.

May 29, 2011 · early morning

The killings

Detective Coy Cox says the crime took place between 1 a.m. and 4 a.m. in the couple's Ridge Edge Court condo. Both are stabbed and suffer blunt force trauma.

May 29, afternoon

The discovery

After the couple miss services at Union Baptist Church, where Bill was a deacon and Peggy played the organ, a family member goes to check and finds them. 911 is called.

May 30, 2011

Person of interest

The Boone County Sheriff's Office releases a photo of the unidentified truck-stop man and names him a person of interest.

2011 – 2012

DNA and prints

A nephew gives fingerprints and DNA voluntarily; neither matches. Cox sends the partial fingerprint to crime labs in all 50 states. In Aug 2012 the KSP lab develops an unidentified DNA profile from one person, found in several places at the scene.

Fall 2012

The Vidocq Society

Experts at the Vidocq Society conclude the ritual-looking elements were likely staged.

June 2023

A fingerprint match

A crime lab technician in a western state reportedly matches the partial fingerprint. Detectives obtain search warrants and collect the man's DNA and fingerprints. The print matches the scene; his DNA does not. (Per a podcast page; not confirmed by the sheriff.)

Aug 2020

Reward and a podcast

The family offers a $50,000 reward. WKRC reports that a podcast, "Just the Tip-Sters with Melissa Morgan," featuring Detective Cox has produced new leads. Cox says the killers targeted both victims and that the scene was altered to look a certain way, adding that "they both had to die." He notes some people wrongly believed the nephew was charged in the Stephensons' deaths; he was charged only in a different local murder.

2023

Reach

A 2023 report says detectives have interviewed more than 250 people in 14 states and found no DNA match.

Latest lead: the 2023 fingerprint match

The most recent reported development is a fingerprint match to an unnamed man whose DNA does not match the crime scene profile.

Not an accusation. The man is unnamed and has not been charged. The information comes from a podcast page and has not been confirmed by the Boone County Sheriff's Office.

What was reported

  • A crime lab technician in a western state reportedly matched the partial fingerprint from the scene to a man.
  • Detectives obtained search warrants and collected his DNA and fingerprints.
  • His fingerprint matched the crime scene; his DNA did not match the unidentified profile.

Why the mismatch matters

  • The print may be innocent. A partial fingerprint can come from a visitor, and a person can leave a print without being the killer.
  • The DNA may be from another person. The unidentified profile is from one person, and there may be more than one offender.
  • Either could be wrong. A lab can err, and a match needs confirming by a second examiner.

What would settle it

  1. Confirm the identification with a second examiner and explain how the man's print came to be there.
  2. Search for a second offender whose DNA matches the profile.
  3. Use genealogy if a better sample can be developed.
Cold Case Checker's assessment

Two good leads that may point to two people

We put the chance that there were two people involved at roughly 40%, since a print and a DNA profile do not match. We put the chance that this case is resolved within five years at roughly 25%, higher than most in this collection, because there is real physical evidence.

A planned, staged killing, possibly by two people, tied to the couple's church or ministry life

  1. This was staged, not a robbery. Valuables were left and the scene was arranged. That is a deliberate effort to mislead.
  2. Time and planning. A postmortem injury about two hours after death means the killer stayed.
  3. The ministry is a likely link. Bill's truck-stop chapel put him in contact with many strangers.

Solved cases

Local and national cases that moved.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
