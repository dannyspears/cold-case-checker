Case file · Jennifer Cohen Case
Updated October 24, 2026Status: unsolved
STATUSNo one has been identified or charged. In October 2026 the NYPD told Brooklyn Paper the investigation is ongoing and the man seen with her remains a person of interest. Crime Stoppers offers up to $3,500. Call 1-800-577-TIPS (anonymous).

Jennifer Cohen case: a man walked into Owl's Head Park with her at 2:40 a.m. and walked out alone. Ten years on, no one knows who he is.

Jennifer Cohen, 35, a Bay Ridge mother of two, was found beaten to death in the skate park at Owl's Head Park, near Colonial Road and Wakeman Place, on the morning of September 29, 2016. Surveillance cameras had recorded her walking down Second Avenue with a man at about 2:45 a.m.; ABC7 later reported that the cameras show the two entering the park together around 2:40 a.m. and the man leaving alone about an hour later. The medical examiner ruled her death a homicide from blunt impact injuries of the head and torso. Police released photos and video of the man within two weeks. He has never been identified.

Cold Case Checker's conclusion · Oct 2026

Case file at a glance

This is one of the rare cold cases with clear video of the last person seen with the victim: his face, his build, his clothes and the way he walks. Journalist Billy Jensen called it his "white whale" and spent thousands of dollars on targeted Facebook ads. Ten years later, the NYPD says the man is still a person of interest. Someone in or near Bay Ridge has likely seen him since.

What happened

Details come from DNAinfo, NBC New York, CBS New York, NY1, News 12, Brooklyn Paper and Bklyner (2016-2017), ABC7's November 2017 investigation, Oxygen (2019), and Brooklyn Paper's ten-year report (Oct 20, 2026).

Before Sept 2016

A Bay Ridge mother

Jennifer, 35, lives in Bay Ridge and has two sons (Brooklyn Paper, 2017). Police later told ABC7 that in her final days she was struggling with drug addiction, had about two dozen mostly misdemeanor arrests, and was engaged in prostitution. Her grandfather told ABC7 he had warned her to stay off the streets at night and called her "my sweetheart."

Late Sept 28 into Sept 29, 2016

The man's walk

Surveillance video traced by ABC7 shows a man on foot starting at 79th Street and Third Avenue, walking more than a mile to 56th Street, then backtracking (ABC7, Nov 2017).

Sept 29 · about 2:40-2:45 a.m.

Walking to the park

He meets Jennifer near 59th Street and Second Avenue. Cameras record the two walking south on Second Avenue toward the park at about 2:45 a.m. (CBS New York; Bklyner) and entering the park together around 2:40 a.m. (ABC7). Outlets differ by a few minutes.

Sept 29 · about 3:40 a.m.

He leaves alone

About an hour after they entered, the man is recorded leaving the park alone (ABC7; Brooklyn Paper, 2026). Det. Agron said he showed no change in demeanor (ABC7).

Sept 29 · about 9-10 a.m.

Found by a jogger

A jogger finds her unconscious with severe head and face trauma, fully clothed, in the skating area of the park near Colonial Road and Wakeman Place; she is pronounced dead at the scene. Reports give 9:00, 9:10, 9:30 and 10 a.m.; Brooklyn Paper says the 911 call came at 10 a.m. Her purse is nearby, with hypodermic needles but no drugs (DNAinfo; Brooklyn Paper; NBC; News 12; Bklyner). ABC7 says she was in the half pipe. Some accounts describe the jogger as an off-duty firefighter (Brooklyn Paper, 2026); we could not confirm that.

Sept 30, 2016

Investigators unsure at first

Sources tell DNAinfo investigators are trying to determine whether she slipped and hit her head or was assaulted. Chief of Detectives Robert Boyce says her pocketbook and some belongings were nearby (DNAinfo).

Oct 1, 2016

Ruled a homicide

The medical examiner's office says she died of blunt impact injuries of the head and torso; the death is a homicide (DNAinfo; NY1).

Oct 2-3, 2016

First video

The NYPD releases surveillance video of the man walking with her on Second Avenue, "less than seven hours" before she was found (NBC New York; CBS New York; Brooklyn Paper). Description: about 5'8", 175 pounds, black jacket with white trim, black shorts with white trim, dark sneakers, dark baseball cap (Brooklyn Paper).

Oct 11-12, 2016

More photos and video

Police release more images and video and add that he carried a black backpack. Police say he has not been described as a suspect, but they want to question him (DNAinfo; Bklyner; NY1; NBC).

Oct 28, 2016

A journalist joins the search

Billy Jensen opens a Facebook page about the man and later spends thousands of dollars on location-targeted Facebook ads (Brooklyn Paper, 2026; Oxygen, 2019).

Mar 2017

Still at large

The Brooklyn Eagle reports her killer has not been found (Brooklyn Eagle; headline only, the site blocks our tools).

Oct 3, 2017

One year

Brooklyn Paper reports the man is still at large and the NYPD offers $2,500. A local historian calls it the park's first murder in nearly 50 years. Her brother's fundraiser for funeral costs had raised $4,700 of $10,000 (Brooklyn Paper).

Nov 3, 2017

The detective's desk

ABC7 traces the man's route on camera. Det. Robert Agron of the 68th Precinct says it is the only homicide he is working, calls it "a brutal scene," and keeps two pictures of her on his desk so she is not forgotten (ABC7).

Mar 21, 2019

"White whale"

Jensen presents the case at a "How to Solve a Murder" event in Manhattan. He says the man has a distinctive walk and ashes cigarettes over sewer grates; tips that he might be Egyptian or seen near halal carts led nowhere (Oxygen). These are Jensen's observations and unverified tips, not police findings.

Oct 20, 2026

Ten years

An NYPD spokesperson tells Brooklyn Paper the investigation is ongoing and the man remains a person of interest; the Crime Stoppers reward is now $3,500. Community Board 10's district manager says $2 million was secured in fiscal 2021 for park renovations including security lighting (Brooklyn Paper).

Cold Case Checker's assessment

The man seen entering the park with her was involved in her death

We put the chance that the man seen with her was involved in her death at roughly 80%: he entered the closed park with her in the middle of the night, left alone an hour later, and never came forward despite wide publicity. That is our judgment, not a finding; he has not been identified or charged, and other explanations remain possible. We put the chance of an arrest within five years at roughly 12%: the video is clear, but ten years of publicity have not produced a name.

Our hypothesis

Editorial reading of public records. A working theory, not a finding, and it names no individual.

Working theory · confidence stated per point

In together, out alone

  1. The timeline is tight. She was alive walking in at about 2:40 a.m.; the only person seen with her left about an hour later.
  2. He has not come forward. His images ran on every New York station within two weeks, and he never identified himself as a witness.
  3. He knew the area. Detectives believe he lived or worked near Bay Ridge, and he walked a long loop through the neighborhood that night.
  4. The video is the best lead. His build, clothes, backpack and walk are on camera; someone who knows him could still recognize him.
  5. Her life put her at risk. Police said she was struggling with addiction and working on the street, which can bring contact with strangers who are hard to trace.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
