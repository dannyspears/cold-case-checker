The Cincinnati Strangler: seven women, one conviction, and six questions.

Between December 1965 and December 1966, seven women were killed in and near Cincinnati's Walnut Hills area. A taxi driver turned laundry worker was convicted in one of the killings and blamed for the rest. Only one case ever went to trial.

Case file at a glance

This is a case where the public story is bigger than the court record. Laskey was convicted of killing Barbara Bowman. The other six were attributed to him largely because the killings stopped after his arrest, and no jury ever heard them.

Victims7 women
PeriodDec 1965 – Dec 1966
PlaceCincinnati, Ohio
ConvictedOne murder (Bowman)
Other sixNever charged
StatusPartly solved
  1. Seven victims in a year.The victims were mostly older women, killed by strangulation or stabbing between Dec 2, 1965 and Dec 9, 1966.
  2. One trial, one conviction.Posteal Laskey Jr. was arrested Dec 9, 1966 and convicted in April 1967 of killing Barbara Bowman. He was sentenced to death; the sentence was changed to life in 1972.
  3. The rest were never tried.He was blamed for the other six because, in investigators' account, the murders stopped after his arrest.
  4. The Bowman case had weak spots.It rested on photo identifications and a taxi that had been reported stolen hours earlier.

Hochhausler is killed on Oct 11; Winstel, 81, is beaten and strangled on Oct 20. The Bricca family is murdered in September, between the fourth and fifth strangling victims.

Lula Kerrick, 81, and the arrest

Kerrick is strangled with her own stocking in an elevator. The same day, Posteal Laskey Jr. is arrested after a witness reports a license plate tied to his car.

April 1967

The trial

Laskey is convicted of Bowman's murder and sentenced to death.

June 1972

Sentence changed

The U.S. Supreme Court changes his sentence to life imprisonment with a chance of parole.

May 29, 2007

Laskey dies

He dies in custody at 69 after repeated parole denials.

Posteal Laskey Jr.

Why named: Arrested Dec 9, 1966 after a witness reported a license plate tied to his car. Convicted in April 1967 of killing Barbara Bowman. Two people later said he had robbed them.

Against: He was never charged with the other six killings. His lawyers argued publicity prevented a fair trial; the court rejected that. The Bowman identification rested on photo lineups and a taxi reported stolen hours earlier.

What would settle it

  1. Retest preserved evidence from all seven cases, if any survives, against Laskey's DNA profile.
  2. Compare methods. The victims were killed by strangulation and by stabbing, which suggests more than one method.
  3. Review the Bowman trial record and the 1966 witness statements.
Cold Case Checker's assessment

One verdict, six open questions

We put the chance that Laskey killed all seven women at roughly 35%. The pattern of older victims and strangulation fits several of the cases, but the Bowman killing, a stabbing of a younger woman, is different. A retest of surviving evidence could change this.

Our hypothesis

Editorial reading of public records. A working theory, not a finding, and it names no individual.

Working theory · confidence stated per point

A real series, an uncertain count, and a conviction that covers only part of it

  1. Several of the killings look related. Older women, strangled, in one part of the city within a year.
  2. The Bowman killing may not fit. A stabbing of a younger woman after a taxi ride differs in method and victim.
  3. The arrest ended the story, not the questions. The series stopped, but that is not proof one person was responsible.
  4. The test is simple. If any evidence survives, DNA could connect or separate the cases.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
