The Amy Mihaljevic case: a phone call, a gift for her mother, and a man who walked her across a parking lot.

On Oct 27, 1989, Amy Mihaljevic, 10, walked from school to the Bay Square Shopping Center in Bay Village, Ohio, to meet a man who had called her. A man led her across the parking lot. Her body was found on Feb 8, 1990 in a farm field in Ashland County. No one has been charged.

Case file at a glance

The caller told Amy he wanted help choosing a gift for her mother's promotion. A jogger found her body three months later. Police and the FBI followed thousands of leads. In 2021 police named a person of interest, and no charge has followed. Three items she wore or carried have never been found.

What happened

Details come from the FBI's page, the Ohio Attorney General listing, Wikipedia, and a July 2026 WOIO report.

Weeks before Oct 27, 1989

The calls

An unknown man phones Amy. He says he works with her mother and wants help choosing a gift for her promotion at a magazine. He asks her to keep it secret and arranges to meet after school.

Oct 27, 1989

The abduction

Amy leaves school on foot and goes to the Bay Square Shopping Center. She is last seen outside a business and appears to be waiting for someone. A man puts his hand on her shoulder and walks her through the parking lot.

Feb 8, 1990

Her body is found

A jogger finds her body in a field next to Township Road 1181 near New London in rural Ashland County. She wore the clothing she was last seen in. The Attorney General listing says she died of stab wounds to the neck and a blow to the head.

2006 to 2008

Other calls and DNA

Other girls in North Olmsted get similar calls. Bay Village police collect DNA from several people. An author names a former science teacher as a possible suspect; no charge follows.

March to Oct 2014

FBI reward

The FBI announces a reward of up to $25,000. Wikipedia says it was raised to $27,000 in October 2014; the FBI's page still says $25,000.

Feb 8, 2021

A person of interest

On the 31st anniversary of the discovery, police announce a man as a person of interest. He is not charged.

June to July 2026

Family appeal

WOIO reports the family and Bay Village police have not given up and are raising funds for DNA work, which the report says remains costly. An Oct 24 5K fundraiser is planned.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
