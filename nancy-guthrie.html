Nancy Guthrie case: an 84-year-old taken from her home in the night, a masked figure at the door, and ransom notes no one can verify.

Nancy Guthrie, 84, the mother of "Today" co-host Savannah Guthrie, was last seen at her home in the Catalina Foothills of Tucson, Arizona, on the evening of Jan 31, 2026. Relatives found her missing the next morning. The FBI and the Pima County Sheriff are investigating it as a kidnapping for ransom. She has a pacemaker, has trouble walking, and needs daily heart medication. She has not been found, and no one has been charged with taking her. This is an urgent, recent, active case: if you know anything, call 1-800-CALL-FBI.

Cold Case Checker's conclusion · Oct 2026

Case file at a glance

This is not a cold case. Investigators believe she was taken from her home overnight. A masked, armed person was filmed at her front door, and blood on her porch matched her DNA. Ransom notes arrived; some were hoaxes, and the FBI says some may be real. The family has been cleared. The most recent developments: a possible new ransom text on Sept 28 was ruled not credible, and a bone fragment found Oct 7 in an Oro Valley wash is with the medical examiner and has not been linked to her.

What happened

Details come from the FBI, PBS NewsHour (AP), NBC News, CBS News, ABC News, Fox News, Today.com, AZPM, KOLD and KGUN, and an ABC7 timeline. Where an item comes only from a lesser outlet, an anonymous source or a retired agent's opinion, we say so. This case is moving, so check the sources below for updates.

Jan 31, 2026 · 5:32 p.m.

Dinner at her daughter's

Nancy Guthrie takes an Uber to her daughter's home for dinner. Investigators speak with the driver.

Jan 31, 2026 · 9:48 p.m.

Home

Family drops her off. Her garage door opens at about 9:48 p.m. and closes at 9:50. The FBI lists this evening as when she was last seen.

Feb 1, 2026 · 1:47 a.m.

The camera goes offline

Her doorbell camera disconnects. At 2:12 a.m. its software detects a person, but no video was saved because the camera had no subscription.

Feb 1, 2026 · 2:28 a.m.

The pacemaker app

Her pacemaker app disconnects from her phone.

Feb 1, 2026 · 11:56 a.m.

Found missing

She does not attend church. Relatives arrive and find her missing. The family calls 911 at 12:03 p.m.

Feb 1 to 2, 2026

Blood on the porch

DNA tests show blood on her front porch matches her (PBS). Before the scene was secured again, journalists photographed blood droplets at the front door (AP). The sheriff later said he probably should have waited longer to hand the home back to the family (AP).

Feb 2, 2026

First ransom note

The sheriff and FBI officials hold a news conference. A note addressed to Savannah Guthrie reaches a Tucson TV station: "Hello Savannah, we have your mother." It demands $4 million in bitcoin by Feb 5, rising to $6 million by 5 p.m. Feb 9. It mentions a white smartwatch at the foot of her bed and a destroyed backyard floodlight, details that led the FBI to take it seriously (CBS; NBC).

Feb 4 to 5, 2026

A hoax

A California man texts the family about a bitcoin payment. He is arrested Feb 5 and later pleads guilty (federal court, via NBC and ABC).

Feb 6, 2026

Second note

A second note to the same station drops the demand. It says she died shortly after being taken, "we believe it was heart related," and that she "is buried in nature now" (CBS; NBC). Investigators cannot confirm it.

Feb 7 to 9, 2026

Deadlines pass

A message to local media demands bitcoin by Feb 9. The family says in a video they would pay and asks for proof of life. The FBI says it is not aware of ongoing contact with the kidnappers (PBS).

Feb 10, 2026

FBI releases images

FBI Director Kash Patel posts images of a masked person with a backpack and a holster at her door, recovered "from residual data located in backend systems" of her camera. A man is detained in a traffic stop south of Tucson, questioned, and released (PBS; AP).

Feb 15 to 17, 2026

A glove and DNA

A glove is found about two miles away. The sheriff says DNA on it did not match anyone in the FBI's CODIS database or DNA from the home (Fox; CNN; NewsNation, seen in search results).

Feb 16, 2026

Family cleared

Sheriff Chris Nanos says the family is cleared as suspects.

Feb 24, 2026

A $1 million reward

The family announces a $1 million reward. Combined with the FBI's, the total tops $1.2 million.

Mar 2 to 4, 2026

The sheriff: "closer"

In an NBC interview Nanos says investigators are "definitely closer," are working on the belief she is alive, and are pursuing thousands of leads. Mixed DNA was found on the property, and some DNA entered in the FBI database got no hits. The backpack is sold exclusively by Walmart, though not necessarily bought there. On Mar 4 he says the glove traced to a restaurant worker who "has nothing to do with the case" (Today).

Apr 16, 2026

New DNA technology

The FBI is analyzing DNA recovered from the home using new technology.

May 10 to 12, 2026

Nothing new

Nanos says "there is really nothing new." A private Florida lab held DNA evidence for 11 weeks before passing it to the FBI. Volunteer groups, including the United Cajun Navy, say the sheriff has not answered their offers to search (Fox).

June 12, 2026

A border-grave tip The sheriff's office says it knows of an anonymous tip claiming she is buried in an unmarked grave near the Mexican border and has not been contacted by Mexican authorities (live blog; not independently confirmed). -Maybe

July 1 to 2, 2026

Fake notes and a plea

An FBI official tells Reuters three notes were fake; the FBI later tells CBS some "may potentially be legitimate." On July 2 the California man pleads guilty to two federal counts (NBC; Yahoo live blog).

July 23, 2026

The FBI and the sheriff disagree

FBI Director Patel says delay by local authorities may have cost cached video from her Nest camera. The sheriff's office says "the FBI was plugged in the night of Guthrie's disappearance" and points to a Feb 2 news conference with FBI officials (AZPM).

July 27 to 31, 2026

Pleas and the notes

Savannah Guthrie posts a video plea on July 27. Nanos says he is "still positive" the case will be solved. On July 30 he tells CBS "I have nothing to indicate she's dead. I don't have a body, a burial site, any note." On July 31 the sheriff's office releases the two notes and asks the public to recognize the writer's wording (Fox; CBS; ABC).

Aug 5, 2026

A slight rise in tips

The sheriff's office says tips rose slightly after the notes were released and that all have been followed up. None has cracked the case (ABC).

Aug 17, 2026

Remains found, not connected

Deputies respond to human remains in the desert near West Ajo Way and South La Cholla Boulevard, about 15 miles from her home. The sheriff's office says there is "no indication" they are connected and that they appeared to have been there for an extended period (live blog, citing CNN and the department's post).

Aug 31, 2026

Hoax sentenced

A federal judge sentences the California man to probation (KOLD and others, seen in search results; reports differ on the length).

Sept 15, 2026

A new plea

On "Today," Savannah Guthrie says the family wants to give their mother "a proper goodbye" (live blog).

Sept 28, 2026

A possible new ransom text

The sheriff's office says the PCSD/FBI task force learned of a possible new ransom text. After review, investigators say it is "not credible." It warns the public not to answer unsolicited messages (Men's Journal; sheriff's posts).

Oct 7, 2026

A bone fragment in Oro Valley

Oro Valley police say a piece of what appears to be a human skull was found in a wash near Vistoso Park and Innovation Park. No other remains were found. The Pima County medical examiner has it. No one has linked it to Nancy Guthrie (KOLD; KGUN).

Oct 20, 2026

More camera stills, reportedly

A lesser outlet, citing sources, reports that additional still images from motion-activated cameras show nothing suspicious and nothing in the overnight window. The sheriff's office says it "continues to analyze various forms of evidence." Not confirmed by an official release. -Maybe

Cold Case Checker's assessment

A planned abduction of a vulnerable person, with noise from opportunists

We put the chance that the abduction was planned, rather than a random burglary, at roughly 85%, because of the disabled camera, the armed person, and the targeting of an elderly woman at night. We put the chance of an arrest within a year at roughly 20%, down from our earlier 25%, because the FBI and the sheriff have large rewards and many leads but no named suspect and no public breakthrough since July.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
