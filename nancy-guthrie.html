The Nancy Guthrie case: an 84-year-old taken from her bed, a masked figure at the door, and ransom notes no one can verify.

Nancy Guthrie, 84, the mother of "Today" co-host Savannah Guthrie, was last seen at her home in the Catalina Foothills of Tucson, Arizona, on the evening of Jan 31, 2026. Relatives found her missing the next morning. The FBI and the Pima County Sheriff are investigating it as a kidnapping for ransom. She has not been found, and no one has been charged.

Cold Case Checker's conclusion · Oct 2026

Case file at a glance

Unlike the other files on this site, this case is recent and still active, and Nancy Guthrie has not been found. Investigators believe she was taken in her sleep. A masked, armed person was filmed at her front door. Many ransom notes arrived and some were hoaxes. The family has been cleared, and the FBI's reward is now up to $100,000 with a family reward of $1 million on top.

What happened

Details come from an ABC7 timeline, NBC reports from July and August 2026, and the FBI's own page. Where an item is only from a lesser outlet we say so. This case is moving, so check the sources below for updates.

Jan 31, 2026 · 5:32 p.m.

Dinner at her daughter's

Nancy Guthrie takes an Uber to her daughter's home for dinner. Investigators speak with the driver.

Jan 31, 2026 · 9:48 p.m.

Home

Family drops her off. Her garage door opens at about 9:48 p.m. and closes at 9:50. The FBI lists this evening as when she was last seen.

Feb 1, 2026 · 1:47 a.m.

The camera goes offline

Her doorbell camera disconnects. At 2:12 a.m. its software detects a person, but no video exists because the camera had no subscription and footage was overwritten.

Feb 1, 2026 · 2:28 a.m.

The pacemaker app

Her pacemaker app disconnects from her phone.

Feb 1, 2026 · 11:56 a.m.

Found missing

Relatives arrive and find her missing. The family calls 911 at 12:03 p.m.

Feb 2 to 9, 2026

Ransom messages

A first note goes to a Tucson TV station on Feb 2. A second, on Feb 6, says she died and "is buried in nature now." On Feb 7 a message to local media demands bitcoin by a Feb 9 deadline. The family says in a video they would pay and asks for proof of life. Investigators say they cannot confirm any of it. One note was traced to an impostor, who was arrested.

Feb 10, 2026

FBI releases video

The FBI releases images of an armed person tampering with the front-door camera. A man is detained for questioning and released without charges.

Feb 15 to Mar 4, 2026

A glove and DNA

An unknown male DNA profile is lifted from a glove found about two miles away. On Mar 4 the sheriff says the glove traced to a restaurant worker who "has nothing to do with the case."

Feb 16, 2026

Family cleared

Sheriff Chris Nanos says the family is cleared as suspects.

Feb 24, 2026

A $1 million reward

The family announces a $1 million reward. ABC7 puts the combined total at $1.2 million. The FBI's reward was $50,000 then and its page now says up to $100,000.

Apr 16, 2026

New DNA technology

The FBI is analyzing DNA recovered earlier from the home using new technology.

July 2, 2026

A guilty plea over a false note

A California man pleads guilty to two federal counts: sending a ransom demand across state lines and using a device to threaten or harass. NBC reports the FBI is still evaluating some notes as legitimate and the case continues as a kidnapping for ransom.

July 31 to Aug 3, 2026

Notes released

The sheriff's office releases the text of two ransom notes and asks the public to help identify the writer. The sheriff urges anyone who knows something to come forward.

Early Oct 2026

Unverified skull report

Lesser outlets report a possible skull fragment found in an Oro Valley wash. We could not confirm this with an official source, and no one has said it relates to her. -Maybe

Cold Case Checker's assessment

A planned abduction of a vulnerable person, with noise from opportunists

We put the chance that the abduction was planned, rather than a random burglary, at roughly 85%, because of the disabled camera and the targeting of an elderly woman at night. We put the chance of an arrest within a year at roughly 25%, because the FBI and the sheriff have many leads and large rewards but no named suspect.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
