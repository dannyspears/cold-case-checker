Mat’e Hines case: a 16-year-old girl found behind an apartment complex on Love Court.

Just before 8 a.m. on Thursday, March 17, 2011, a neighbor found 16-year-old Mat’e Hines lying behind an apartment complex in the 10000 block of Love Court in Lincoln Heights, a village north of Cincinnati in Hamilton County. Police said she apparently died of a gunshot wound to the head. We found no arrest.

Someone she knew or was with that night

Working theory · confidence stated per point

Someone she was with that night

  1. She was found outside, behind a residential complex. It is not a street corner or a business; it is a place where people who live or visit there walk.
  2. She had family in the village. Her grandmother lived in Lincoln Heights, so she likely knew people in the area.
  3. Nothing suggests a robbery. No report mentions anything taken; none says either way.
  4. The night is the gap. The hours before she was found have never been described publicly. Whoever she was with that evening is the missing link.
  5. Teenagers talk. Friends at school or in the neighborhood may have heard something in 2011 that they were afraid to share then. They are adults now.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
