Case file · Alan Holcomb Case
Updated November 7, 2026Status: open, unsolved
STATUSThe Ohio Attorney General lists the case as unsolved homicide 3903. No arrest has been reported and police have named no suspect. Rewards of $2,500 from his family and $2,500 from Crime Stoppers are listed. Call the Cincinnati Police Homicide Unit at 513-352-3542 or Crime Stoppers at 513-352-3040 (anonymous).

Alan Holcomb case: he watched the fireworks with his mother and came home. By morning he lay shot in a yard near their building, and his family is still shocked no one heard the gunshot.

On the night of July 4, 2023, Alan Holcomb, 39, watched fireworks with his mother, and she dropped him off at the Williamsburg Apartments in Hartwell, where the two had shared an apartment for more than 20 years. She went to get food. When she came back she did not see him and assumed he had gone to bed. About 8 a.m. on July 5, police were called to the 5300 block of Kingsway Court and found him shot to death in a yard at the complex.

Case file at a glance

His mother learned what had happened when she woke to crime scene tape outside and could not leave for work. His family says he was a homebody with no enemies, and they are still shocked that no one heard a gunshot. Police have not said when he was shot or why. More than three years on, no arrest has been reported. His family has added $2,500 to the Crime Stoppers reward. "Somebody knows something," his aunt said.

What happened

Details come from WKRC and WCPO (July 2023), the family's one-year and two-year appeals on FOX19, WKRC and WCPO, the Ohio Attorney General listing, and his obituary. The account of the night comes from his family, not from police. WCPO pages show no date, so their dates are worked out from the text.

July 4, 2023

A call with his father

His father, Dr. Alvin Holcomb, who lives in New York, talks with him before he and his mother go to watch the fireworks (FOX19, 2025).

July 4, 2023 · night

Fireworks

He watches fireworks with his mother, at Ault Park according to FOX19 (2024).

About midnight

Dropped off

His mother drops him off at the apartment and goes to get food, his aunt says (FOX19; WKRC; WCPO, 2024).

Later that night

She comes home

She does not see him on the first floor and assumes he has gone to the basement to sleep (FOX19, 2024). WCPO and WKRC say she assumed he had gone to bed.

July 5, 2023 · about 8 a.m.

Police are called

Officers go to the 5300 block of Kingsway Court for a person shot and find him dead in a yard at the Williamsburg Apartments (WKRC; WCPO). FOX19 quotes police as saying about 9 a.m. Who called has not been published.

July 5, 2023 · morning

Crime scene tape

His mother wakes to police and tape that keep her from leaving for work. Not long after, police tell her they are investigating her son's murder, his aunt says (WCPO, 2024). His father gets the call around midday (FOX19, 2025).

July 6, 2023

Identified

The Hamilton County Coroner identifies him. Police release no suspect information (WCPO; WKRC).

July 22, 2023

Funeral

Services are held at Arlington Memorial Gardens, where he is buried (obituary).

Aug 2024

Cold case task force

Cincinnati police and Hamilton County leaders launch a cold case task force. We do not know whether it has reviewed this case (FOX19).

July 5, 2024

One year: a reward

The family holds a memorial and news conference and adds $2,500 to the $2,500 from Crime Stoppers. Police have arrested no one and named no suspect (WKRC; WCPO; FOX19).

July 2025

Two years

The family appeals again. WCPO: "No suspects were ever arrested in his murder." His father says he was not a confrontational person (FOX19, Jul 8, 2025).

Sept 2026

Police blitz

Cincinnati police hand out flyers in a four-day push on unsolved homicides. FOX19's report does not mention this case.

Nov 2026

Our check

The state still lists the case as unsolved. We found no arrest or charge and no report newer than July 2025. We have not confirmed the status with police.

Died

Alan Davis Holcomb, 39

Born June 9, 1984, in Flushing, Queens, to Debora Wilson and Dr. Alvin Holcomb (obituary). He was his mother's only child and shared an apartment with her for more than 20 years (WKRC; WCPO). His family calls him a homebody, always looking out for others and willing to help. He and his aunt, Kisha Ivory, talked San Francisco 49ers on Sundays; she still listens to his old voicemails (WCPO). His father says he had a kind and generous heart (FOX19). A contributed memorial profile, which we could not verify, says he graduated from Withrow High School, attended Cincinnati State and Northern Kentucky University, and worked at Tyson Foods.

What would settle it

  1. The hour. Anyone at the complex that night who heard a shot, or saw him outside, after midnight.
  2. A camera. Doorbell, dash or parking-lot video from Kingsway Court, kept from that night.
  3. His phone. Who called or texted him after he got home, and why he went outside.
  4. Talk. Anyone who has heard someone discuss it. His cousin says, "I'm certain that someone knows something."
Cold Case Checker's assessment

Someone he met outside the complex after midnight, on a night when gunfire could pass for fireworks

We put the chance that he was shot during the night of July 4-5, rather than close to dawn, at roughly 70%. We put the chance that he knew or recognized the shooter at roughly 50%: his family knows of no enemies, but nothing public rules it out. We put the chance of a charge within five years at roughly 15%. These are our judgments, not findings.

Our hypothesis

Editorial reading of public records. A working theory, not a finding, and it names no individual.

Working theory · confidence stated per point

He stepped outside after midnight, and the holiday covered the sound

  1. He left the apartment on his own, or never went in. His mother did not see him when she came back, and he was found outside. No report mentions a break-in.
  2. Something brought a homebody outdoors. A call, a text, a noise or a person. That is why his phone records matter.
  3. The fireworks may have hidden the shot. This is our idea, not a police statement. On July 4 night a gunshot can pass for fireworks, which could explain why his family says no one heard it.
  4. He lay there for hours. If so, the shooter had time to get away and no one raised the alarm until morning.
  5. People nearby may know more than they think. His mother has said she knows it can be hard to come forward, and the family raised the reward for that reason.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
