Brian Thompson case: a father home from a store run, shot in front of his house with his hands up.

On Sunday night, June 24, 2012, Brian J. Thompson, 27, came home to Storrs Street in Lower Price Hill from a store run; a community group says he had gone for formula for his infant. He got out of his car and heard gunshots. His mother says he told people to get a baby and a neighbor inside, then raised his hands as gunmen came to his car window, and was shot in the face. He died in front of his house. Two other people were wounded down the street and survived. No one has been charged.

Case file at a glance

Accounts of why it happened differ. WLWT's headline called it a street robbery; Santa Maria Community Services says people tried to take his car; an independent producer says the detective told him Brian was an innocent bystander caught in a shooting after a dice game he had nothing to do with. His mother said in 2015 that people in the neighborhood know who did it but were paid off or scared into silence.

Three shot

Identified

FOX19 and WCPO name him; WCPO describes him as the father of two.

Jun 2012

His fiancee speaks

WLWT reports that his fiancee witnessed the killing and says he was "in the wrong place at the wrong time." Its headline calls it a street robbery (headline and summary; we could not open the story).

Jul 2, 2012

Neighbors meet

Lower Price Hill residents ask city council members to help stop the violence, calling his death the latest incident; District Three officers attend (FOX19).

Jul 2012

Santa Maria Community Services holds the first Brian Thompson Memorial Fishing Tournament; it becomes an annual event for neighborhood families.

Jun 24, 2015

"I'm begging as a mother"

His mother tells WKRC that people in the neighborhood know who killed him but were paid off or scared, and that some who know are not from the neighborhood. Crime Stoppers offers up to $2,000 for information leading to a conviction.

Cold Case Checker's assessment

A shooting that grew out of a dispute nearby, by gunmen known in the neighborhood

We put the chance that the gunmen were known to people on Storrs Street at roughly 75%, because his mother says neighbors know who did it and the detective reportedly tied it to a dice game. That is our judgment, not a finding. We put the chance of a charge within five years at roughly 10%: witnesses exist, but none has come forward publicly.

Working theory · confidence stated per point

A neighborhood shooting with people who saw it

  1. Three accounts, one street. Robbery, carjacking, or a dispute after a dice game: all put the gunmen on Storrs Street that night, close to home.
  2. He was a bystander. He had just come home from a store and was protecting others, by his mother's account.
  3. Three people were shot. That suggests shooting at a group, not a single targeted robbery.
  4. There are witnesses. Two survivors, his fiancee, and neighbors his mother says know who did it.
  5. Fear kept people quiet. His mother says some were paid off or scared. Years later, some may be ready to talk.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
