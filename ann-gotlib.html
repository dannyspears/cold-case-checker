Ann Gotlib case: a 12-year-old immigrant, a mall, a bike left behind, and a suspect who died before he could be charged.

On June 1, 1983, Ann Gotlib, 12, who had come to the United States from Ukraine three years earlier, was last seen at Bashford Manor Mall in Louisville, Kentucky. Her red-and-white bicycle was found outside a department store. She has never been found. This is a missing-person case, and no one has been arrested or charged.

Latest lead: the 2008 statement and what could confirm it

The case turns on whether the 2008 conclusion can be backed with evidence.

Not an accusation. The man named by police in 2008 was never charged and is deceased.

What is known

  • Police named a deceased man in 2008.
  • No remains have been found.

What would settle it

  1. Locate remains where the man lived or traveled in 1983.
  2. Test any retained evidence against his DNA, if samples exist.
  3. Review the cellmate's statement against known facts only the killer would know.
Cold Case Checker's assessment

An abduction by someone who approached her at the mall

We put the chance that she was abducted at the mall at roughly 90%. We put the chance that the man police named was involved at roughly 55%, because the evidence is a cellmate's report and a polygraph. We put the chance that she is found within five years at roughly 4%.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
