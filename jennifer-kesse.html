Jennifer Kesse case: a young finance manager gone before work, and a stranger on camera walking away from her car.

Jennifer Joyce Kesse, 24, spoke with her boyfriend just before 10 p.m. on January 23, 2006, from her new condominium off Conroy Road near the Mall at Millenia in Orlando. The next morning she did not arrive at work at Westgate Resorts. Inside her home, a damp towel and laid-out clothes suggested she had showered and dressed. Her black 2004 Chevrolet Malibu was found on January 26 at the Huntington on the Green condominiums about a mile away. A security camera had recorded someone parking it around noon on January 24 and walking off. That person has never been identified, and Jennifer has never been found.

Case file at a glance

Orlando police released the video in May 2007 and began calling the person in it a suspect. A $1 million reward for her safe return went unclaimed. What happened

Details come from the FBI ViCAP poster, FDLE's January 2026 release, Fox News (May 2007), WFTV (Dec 2018), ClickOrlando (Oct 2025) and the Wikipedia summary of the case and its sources.

May 20, 1981

Born

Jennifer Joyce Kesse is born in New Jersey and raised in Tampa. She earns a finance degree from the University of Central Florida in 2003 (Wikipedia).

Jan 2006

A new start

She has just bought a condominium, received a promotion at Westgate Resorts in Ocoee, and returned from a Caribbean vacation (Fox News, 2007).

Jan 23, 2006 · evening

Last calls

She leaves work about 6 p.m. and calls her father on the drive home. Her last known call, to her boyfriend, is at about 9:56 p.m. (Fox News; Wikipedia).

Jan 24, 2006 · morning

Gone

She does not arrive at work or make her usual morning call. A damp towel and laid-out clothes suggest she had showered and dressed. Her phones, iPod, keys, driver's license and purse are gone (FBI; Wikipedia). Police later put the window between 10 p.m. on Jan 23 and 8 a.m. on Jan 24 (WFTV).

Jan 24, 2006 · about noon

The car is parked

A security camera at the Huntington on the Green condominiums records a person parking her Malibu and walking away. A fence blocks the face in every frame (Fox News; Wikipedia).

Jan 26, 2006

Car found

Her black 2004 Chevrolet Malibu is found about a mile from her home (FDLE; WFTV).

May 2007

Video released

Orlando police release the surveillance video and now call the person in it a suspect. The FBI estimates the person's height at 5'3" to 5'5". A $1 million reward is offered for her safe return until July 4 (Fox News).

May 2008

A state law

Florida passes the Jennifer Kesse and Tiffany Sessions Missing Persons Act (Wikipedia).

Apr 2010

Leads exhausted

According to the family's later lawsuit, police acknowledged in April 2010 that they had exhausted all leads but said the case remained active (WFTV, 2018).

Dec 11, 2018

Family sues

Her parents sue the Orlando Police Department for the case file. Police had offered a heavily redacted copy for about $18,000 (WFTV).

Mar 2019

Settlement

The suit settles, and the family receives about 16,000 pages of documents (Wikipedia).

2022

FDLE takes over

The Florida Department of Law Enforcement takes over the investigation (FDLE).

Jul 2023

FBI alert

The FBI's Violent Criminal Apprehension Program reissues its alert with her photo and the surveillance image (FBI).

Oct 22, 2025

"Not cold anymore"

Her father says FDLE found DNA on evidence never tested before, narrowed the persons of interest to a few, and told the family the case is no longer cold. A documentary is in development (ClickOrlando).

Jan 24, 2026

Twenty years

FDLE says it has done more than 115 interviews and reviewed tens of thousands of documents; its Orlando special agent in charge says the focus is "finding Jennifer and bringing justice in this case" (FDLE).

What would settle it

  1. A DNA match from the newly tested evidence or the car.
  2. An identification of the person in the video, by gait, clothing or new image processing.
  3. The first hours. Anyone near her condo or the Huntington on the Green lot on the morning of January 24, 2006.
Cold Case Checker's assessment

Taken near her home that morning by someone who then drove her car away

We put the chance that she was taken by someone from her immediate surroundings, rather than a stranger passing through, at roughly 60%, because the car was left close to her home by someone who seemed to know the area. That is our judgment, not a finding. We put the chance of a charge within five years at roughly 20%, higher than most cases here, because FDLE reports new DNA and a short list of persons of interest.

Our hypothesis

Editorial reading of public records. A working theory, not a finding, and it names no individual.

Working theory · confidence stated per point

Taken on the way to work

  1. She got ready for work. The towel and laid-out clothes suggest she was taken after showering, likely while leaving home that morning.
  2. Her car moved only a mile. It was left at a nearby complex, not abandoned far away.
  3. The driver walked away. Whoever parked it left on foot, which suggests they knew the area or had a way out.
  4. Nothing of hers has surfaced. No phone, card or belongings in twenty years.
  5. FDLE has new DNA. A match to anyone in a database would change everything.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
