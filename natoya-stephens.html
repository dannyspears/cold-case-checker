Natoya Stephens case: a Brooklyn mother of two gone after a morning argument at home, and fourteen years of near silence.

Natoya Stephens, 29, was last seen at about 7 a.m. on July 10, 2012, in the 500 block of Lefferts Avenue in Brooklyn, inside the NYPD's 71st Precinct, according to the police flyer. She lived there with her fiance, and accounts say the two had argued before she vanished. She was the mother of two children and co-owned a restaurant on Fulton Street with him. Police put out an alert on July 20 and asked the public for help on July 24. A month later her family said her fiance would not tell them anything about her disappearance. She has never been heard from again.

Last seen

Reported missing (unverified)

Jul 20, 2012

Police flyer

The NYPD issues missing-person circular 12/147 (Missing Persons Squad case 12/7623): female, Black, 29, 5'1", 130 pounds, medium build, black hair, brown eyes, flower tattoo on her hand; clothing unknown. Contacts: the Missing Persons Squad and Detectives Santos and Coffey of the 71st Squad (NYPD flyer).

Jul 21, 2012

Neighborhood alert

A local blog, The Q at Parkside, posts the 71st Precinct's "missing person emergency" with the flyer. A reader shares it on the Prospect Lefferts Gardens Facebook page (The Q at Parkside).

Jul 24, 2012

Police ask for help

News 12 Brooklyn reports that police are asking the public for help finding her, two weeks after she was last seen at her Prospect Lefferts Gardens home (News 12).

Late Jul 2012

"We're thinking kidnapping"

NY1 interviews her family, according to a blog summary posted Aug 1: a family friend says the family is considering kidnapping, and her daughter says she hopes her mother is safe and needed time to clear her head. That summary places the home in Canarsie, which conflicts with the police flyer (Missing Persons of America, citing NY1; we could not find the NY1 report).

Aug 22, 2012

Family pleads for help

News 12 Brooklyn reports that, more than a month later, her family is pleading with the public. Relatives say she owns Ricky's Eat Well, a restaurant on Fulton Street, that her fiance has been running it since she went missing, and that he will not tell them anything about her disappearance (News 12; the restaurant name and the family's statement come from the report's text in search results, because we could read only its opening).

Apr 2014

A friend's page

Her best friend runs a Facebook page, "Finding Natoya Stephens, a missing Bklyn mom of two," and keeps asking people to share it (quoted by Our Black Girls; we cannot read Facebook).

Aug 3, 2018

Charley Project

The Charley Project adds her case, noting a flower tattoo and the name "Ricky" on her left hand and pierced ears. It says "few details are available" (Charley Project).

Dec 2020 to Feb 2021

Case profiles

Uncovered (from NamUs) and True Case Files publish profiles. True Case Files notes her fiance has not been publicly named as a suspect (Uncovered; True Case Files).

2021

Her best friend writes that the detectives need to revisit the case (quoted by Our Black Girls).

Mar 3, 2022

Named in Congress

Opening a House Oversight subcommittee hearing on missing women and girls of color, Rep. Carolyn Maloney cites Natoya, "a 29-year-old Black mom of two," missing since 2012 after an argument with her fiance, and says her loved ones are still seeking information (Maloney statement; hearing record).

What would settle it

  1. A DNA or tattoo match through NamUs. Her family can give DNA to NamUs if they have not already done so.
  2. Someone who saw her on July 10, 2012, on Lefferts Avenue, on the way to Fulton Street, or anywhere else that day.
  3. Someone she confided in before she vanished about the relationship, money, or plans to leave.
Cold Case Checker's assessment

She did not leave on her own and came to harm soon after July 10, 2012

We put the chance that she did not leave voluntarily at roughly 85%: she left two children and a business, her family and best friend say it is unlike her, and nothing of hers has surfaced in fourteen years. That is our judgment, not a finding, and it names no one. We put the chance that the case is resolved within five years at roughly 8%, because there are few public leads and no reported forensic evidence.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
