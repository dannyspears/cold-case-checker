The Rhoda Nathan case: a wrongful-conviction reversal that reopened the question of who killed a visiting grandmother.

On the morning of Sept 3, 1994, Rhoda Nathan, 67, of Toms River, New Jersey, in Blue Ash for a bar mitzvah, was found badly injured in her room at the Embassy Suites hotel. She died that day. A hotel employee was convicted and sentenced to death in 1996 to 1997. In 2022 a judge vacated the convictions and in December 2025 the prosecutor dismissed the case.

Cold Case Checker's conclusion · Oct 2026

Case file at a glance

The conviction rested on a pendant, a hand injury and expert opinions. A judge found that more than 4,000 pages of police files had not been disclosed, including accounts of other people at the hotel. DNA did not implicate him or any alternative suspect. The Hamilton County prosecutor dismissed the case and said she is not convinced he killed Rhoda Nathan.

What happened

Details come from the National Registry of Exonerations, Spectrum News and WCPO. We do not name the man who was convicted; the charge against him was dismissed.

Sept 3, 1994 · morning

Found in her room

Rhoda Nathan, 67, a grandmother from Toms River, NJ, in town for a bar mitzvah, is found nude and badly injured on the floor of her room at the Embassy Suites in Blue Ash. She dies later that day. Her pendant is missing and money is gone from her purse.

1994

A single suspect

A 42-year-old hotel employee with a prior record becomes the sole focus. Police point to a hand injury he reported that day and a pendant they say was in a toolbox in his car. He denies involvement.

Nov 26, 1996

Convicted

He is convicted of aggravated murder and aggravated robbery after a trial that relied on the pendant, the hand wound, and expert comparisons.

Jan 9, 1997

Sentenced to death

The judge imposes the sentence the jury recommended.

2016

An unverified account

A woman tells a fellow inmate that her husband admitted the killing and framed another man. The registry reports it; we could not confirm it. -Maybe

Dec 20, 2022

New trial ordered

A Hamilton County judge vacates the convictions, finding more than 4,000 pages of police files were not disclosed, including a hepatitis B finding in the autopsy, accounts of two men leaving the victim's room, and a guest's account of a man fleeing a side door. He is freed.

2025 (date not stated)

The hepatitis B dispute

The prosecutor announces modern testing excludes him. The coroner issues and then withdraws a statement about a lab report. The dispute is about the victim's test, which the defense says supports their position. -Maybe

Dec 12, 2025

Case dismissed

Hamilton County Prosecutor Connie Pillich dismisses the case and says she is not convinced he killed Rhoda Nathan. The registry lists him as exonerated.

July 30, 2026

Civil lawsuit

The freed man sues the city of Blue Ash and former officers. Local headlines say the suit alleges evidence was planted and that officers had tunnel vision. These are allegations in a lawsuit, not findings, and we have not read the complaint. -Maybe

What is known

  • The registry says DNA testing did not implicate the convicted man or any alternative suspect.

What would settle it

  1. Re-open the investigation with the 4,000 pages of files that were not disclosed.
  2. Run the DNA through genetic genealogy and CODIS.
  3. Find the two men and the guest's man described in the hotel accounts.
Cold Case Checker's assessment

A hotel burglary-robbery by someone other than the man convicted

We put the chance that the killer was a non-employee who entered the room at roughly 40%, a hotel worker other than the convicted man at roughly 30%, and the convicted man at roughly 10%, given the judge's findings, the DNA result and the dismissal. These are not findings and the remaining 20% covers other scenarios.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
