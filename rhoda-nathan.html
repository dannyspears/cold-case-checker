Rhoda Nathan case: a grandmother killed before a bar mitzvah, and a death-row conviction that fell apart.

On the morning of Saturday, Sept 3, 1994, Rhoda Nathan, 67, a widow from Toms River, New Jersey, stayed behind to get ready while the friends sharing her room at the Embassy Suites in Blue Ash went down to breakfast. They were in town for her best friend's grandson's bar mitzvah. When they came back, she was on the floor, badly beaten. She died that day. A hotel handyman was convicted and sentenced to death; a judge vacated the conviction in 2022, and in December 2025 the prosecutor dismissed the case, saying her office had been wrong.

Cold Case Checker's conclusion · Oct 2026

Case file at a glance

The conviction rested on a pendant, a hand injury and expert opinions. A judge found that more than 4,000 pages of police files had not been disclosed, including accounts of other people at the hotel. The prosecutor dismissed the case with prejudice, the former prosecutor who tried it publicly defended his office, and Rhoda's son says the right man was convicted. Whoever killed her, no one is charged today.

What happened

Details come from the National Registry of Exonerations, Spectrum News, WCPO, WKRC, FOX19, WVXU, New Jersey 101.5, the Ohio Supreme Court, and a Forensic Files fan summary (labeled where used). We do not name the man who was convicted; the charge against him was dismissed.

Jan 15, 1927

Born

Rhoda Silverman is born in the Bronx. She later marries, raises two sons, and by 1994 is a widow in a retirement community in Toms River, New Jersey, who loves theater, tennis, golf and travel (Forensic Files fan summary; unverified).

Labor Day weekend, 1994

A bar mitzvah

She flies to Ohio for the bar mitzvah of her best friend's grandson and shares a suite at the Embassy Suites in Blue Ash with her friend and another companion (New Jersey 101.5; registry).

Sept 3, 1994 · morning

Found in her room

Her roommates go down to breakfast to give her time to get ready. When they return, she is on the floor, nude and badly injured; a doctor and a nurse staying at the hotel try to help. She dies later that day. A pendant is missing and money is gone from a purse in the room (registry; fan summary for the doctor and nurse).

1994

A single suspect

A 42-year-old hotel handyman with a prior record becomes the sole focus. Police point to a hand injury and a pendant they say was in a toolbox in his car. He denies involvement.

Nov 26, 1996

Convicted

He is convicted of aggravated murder, aggravated robbery and burglary after a trial that relied on the pendant, the hand wound, and expert comparisons.

Jan 9, 1997

Sentenced to death

The judge imposes the sentence the jury recommended. His execution is later scheduled six times (WKRC).

2016

An unverified account

A woman tells a fellow inmate that her husband admitted the killing and framed another man. The registry reports it; we could not confirm it.

Dec 20, 2022

New trial ordered

A Hamilton County judge vacates the convictions, finding more than 4,000 pages of police files were not disclosed, including a hepatitis B finding in the autopsy, accounts of two men leaving the victim's room, and a guest's account of a man fleeing a side door.

Jan 2023

Released

He is released on bond after 9,971 days in prison to await a retrial (WKRC).

Dec 4, 2025

Ohio Supreme Court

The court sends the State's request to appeal the new-trial order back to the appeals court for a fuller review (2025-Ohio-5389).

Dec 12, 2025

Case dismissed

Prosecutor Connie Pillich dismisses the case with prejudice, so it can never be refiled, citing no physical evidence tying him to the killing and alternative suspects never investigated. "The fact of the matter is, this office was wrong" (Spectrum News; WVXU; FOX19).

Dec 17, 2025

The former prosecutor responds

Ohio Supreme Court Justice Joe Deters, who prosecuted the case in the 1990s, calls the claims that his office hid evidence offensive and says the withheld material was insignificant (WKRC).

Dec 20, 2025

Her son speaks

Rhoda's son tells FOX19 he has "not a doubt" the convicted man is guilty and that his release is "disgusting."

Dec 22, 2025

The hepatitis B reversal

The coroner says a lab report shows Rhoda was negative for hepatitis B, undercutting a key defense point. Defense lawyers show she had read the convicted man's report; the coroner then confirms Rhoda's own report was positive and thanks them for the correction (WKRC; WCPO).

July 30, 2026

Federal lawsuit

The freed man sues Blue Ash and the officers who investigated. The suit alleges a bloody handprint was not preserved, other suspects and a confession were ignored, and the pendant appeared only on a second search of his car. These are allegations, not findings; the city says it will respond in court (WKRC).

Latest: a dismissal, a disputed record, and a lawsuit

The most important development is not a new suspect. It is that the long-accepted answer has been withdrawn, and the people closest to the case disagree about it.

Not an accusation. No one is named here.

What is known

  • The charge was dismissed with prejudice on Dec 12, 2025; the prosecutor says her office was wrong.
  • The former prosecutor who tried the case, now a state Supreme Court justice, defends the conviction; so does Rhoda's son.
  • The coroner confirmed Rhoda's own hepatitis B test was positive after briefly saying otherwise.
  • A federal lawsuit filed July 2026 accuses the original investigators of tunnel vision; it has not been decided.

What would settle it

  1. Re-open the investigation with the 4,000 pages of files that were not disclosed.
  2. Run the DNA through genetic genealogy and CODIS.
  3. Find the two men and the guest's man described in the hotel accounts.
  4. Discovery in the lawsuit may put more of the 1994 record into public view.
Cold Case Checker's assessment

A hotel burglary-robbery by someone other than the man convicted

We put the chance that the killer was a non-employee who entered the room at roughly 40%, a hotel worker other than the convicted man at roughly 30%, and the convicted man at roughly 10%, given the judge's findings, the DNA result and the dismissal. Her son disagrees, and these are not findings; the remaining 20% covers other scenarios.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
