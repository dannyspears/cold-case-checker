The Gene Gilbert case: a repairman who vanished from Maysville and a van left across the river.

Permm Gilbert, 46, a long-time traveling appliance repairman, was last seen in Maysville, Kentucky on May 22, 1982. His nude body was found the next morning in a ditch on Swope Road in Clermont County, Ohio, and his work van turned up days later in Adams County. Investigators say robbery was not the motive.

Case file at a glance

The sheriff's own write-up says the killing probably centered on events in Maysville and that someone may have held a grudge. What happened

Details come mainly from a 2012 Clermont Sun article by the sheriff's chief deputy. Names are spelled "Permm" in that article and "Permon" in other reports.

May 22, 1982 · before noon

Last seen

Gilbert, a traveling repairman who worked Brown, Clermont and Adams counties and parts of northern Kentucky, is last seen at a grocery on Forest Avenue in Maysville, Kentucky, after a service call.

May 23, 1982

The discovery

His nude body is found in a low-lying ditch along Swope Road in Clermont County, within about five miles of his home on Ohio 774. He had been shot. His family had reported him missing.

Several days later

The van

His white panel work van is found abandoned on Ebenezer Road in Adams County, across the river from Maysville. Investigators say it yielded evidence that modern technology could analyze.

Nov 2011

A new look

The sheriff assigns the case to the Investigative Section for in-depth follow-up.

Mar 30, 2012

Public appeal

The sheriff's chief deputy writes that investigators have identified several persons of interest, that robbery was not the motive, and that a grudge connected to Maysville is possible. He asks for tips.

Later (undated)

What is known

  • The sheriff's chief deputy wrote in 2012 that robbery was not the motive and that the killing likely centered on events in Maysville.
  • The van held evidence that investigators said modern technology could analyze.
  • A later local item says a recent test found nothing new. We have not seen it in full.

What would settle it

  1. Re-test the van and clothing for DNA and for genetic genealogy.
  2. Interview people in Maysville who knew his customers and his last calls.
  3. Pull his customer list and service tickets for the week before May 22, 1982.
Cold Case Checker's assessment

A targeted killing tied to someone on his Maysville route

We put the chance that the killer knew him or his route at roughly 70%, which matches the sheriff's grudge theory. We put the chance of a solution within five years at roughly 8%, because the newest test reportedly found nothing.

Our hypothesis

Editorial reading of public records. A working theory, not a finding, and it names no individual.

Working theory · confidence stated per point

A personal killing with the body dumped near home

  1. It was not a robbery. The sheriff says so.
  2. The dump site is close to home. The body was left about five miles from his house, which suggests the killer knew the area or him.
  3. The van was used and left. Moving it across the river bought time.
  4. Maysville holds the answer. The last sighting and the sheriff's theory both point there.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
