Ryan Realbuto: a 23-year-old volunteer walking home from church told robbers he had no money. One of them got out of the car and shot him.

Ryan Realbuto, 23, of Pittsford, New York, had come to Washington in August 2023 with the Capuchin Franciscan Volunteer Corps to work with students at Don Bosco Cristo Rey High School. On Thursday, January 18, 2024, he and two fellow volunteers went to a Holy Hour and young adults' gathering at Immaculate Conception Church in Shaw, then took the Metro to Fort Totten and walked toward the house they shared. At about 10:04 p.m., in the 5000 block of South Dakota Avenue NE, a car pulled up and someone inside demanded money. When the three said they had none, a man got out and shot Ryan in the stomach. The robbers drove off without taking anything. He died at a hospital. Police said they were looking for a dark-colored Honda Accord. No one has been arrested.

Cold Case Checker's assessment

A random robbery crew in a car

We put the chance that this was a robbery by strangers who did not know him at roughly 95%: the account of all three witnesses and police agree. That is our judgment, not a finding. We put the chance of an arrest within five years at roughly 25%: armed robbery crews often reoffend, and a gun or car can link cases.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
