Case file · Christian Moretti Case
Updated October 26, 2026Status: unsolved
STATUSNo arrest or charge found as of Oct 2026. Police called it a targeted killing. Call the Youngstown Police Detective Bureau at 330-742-8911 or Crime Stoppers at 330-746-CLUE (2583); tips can be anonymous.

Christian Moretti case: someone followed his SUV onto I-680 at rush hour and fired a rifle into it. Two years on, no one has been charged.

Christian Moretti, 20, was driving north on Interstate 680 in Youngstown at about 4 p.m. on Tuesday, August 20, 2024, when someone in a passing car fired several shots at his SUV between the Glenwood Avenue and Salt Springs Road exits. The SUV crashed into a concrete barrier; he was alone and died at the scene. Police recovered casings from a semiautomatic rifle along a half-mile stretch of freeway and said the pattern showed the attack was targeted, not random. The lead detective said he believed the shooter had followed the SUV for some time. Police have released no description of the shooter or the car, and we found no arrest.

Cold Case Checker's conclusion · Oct 2026

Case file at a glance

This happened on one of the busiest roads in the Mahoning Valley, in daylight, at rush hour. Other drivers were all around. Someone saw the car the shots came from, and some of them may have had dash cameras running. Police asked anyone who was on I-680 that afternoon to call.

What happened

Details come from WKBN (Aug 20-22, 2024), WFMJ (Aug 22, 2024), The Vindicator (Aug 22-23, 2024 and Jan 18, 2025) and the obituaries published by the family. Reports differ on exact spots along the freeway; we note each version.

Born

The funeral home lists his birth date as September 25, 2003; one version of the Vindicator obituary gives August 25, 2003 (Becker Funeral Homes; Vindicator).

2022-2023

Losses

His obituary says he was preceded in death by his mother (2022) and his father (2023) (Becker Funeral Homes).

Aug 20, 2024 · about 4 p.m.

Shots on I-680

Driving north on I-680 in afternoon rush hour, his SUV is fired on by someone in a passing car between the Glenwood Avenue and Salt Springs Road exits (WKBN). WFMJ reports police believe he was shot near the Glenwood and Mahoning Avenue exit.

Aug 20 · 4:13 p.m.

The crash

Police are notified at 4:13 p.m. near northbound exit 4A (U.S. 422 / Route 193) (Vindicator). The SUV hits a concrete barrier in the middle of the freeway (WKBN), a few hundred feet north of the Marshal Street entrance ramp (WKBN, Aug 22); WFMJ says it stopped near the Mahoning Avenue bridge. He is alone and dies at the scene.

Aug 20 · 4:41 p.m.

Coroner called

The coroner's office is notified at 4:41 p.m. Detectives, crime scene investigators and the coroner take over the scene (Vindicator).

Aug 20 · evening

A half-mile scene

Lt. Mohammad Awad says the casing pattern showed the shooting was not random; police recover semiautomatic rifle casings and he believes the shooter followed the SUV for some time. The scene stretches half a mile; extra crime scene staff and detectives search both directions for evidence and witnesses. Northbound I-680 is closed for more than an hour; WFMJ says until about 6:30 p.m. (WKBN; WFMJ). Mayor Tito Brown calls the location a concern but says it "was not a random act" (WKBN).

Aug 22, 2024

Identified

The Mahoning County Coroner's Office identifies him as Christian Moretti, 20; the autopsy is at the Cuyahoga County Medical Examiner's Office. Police say two detectives are "working around the clock"; WFMJ reports no arrests but "some leads" (WKBN; WFMJ; Vindicator).

Late Aug 2024

Private services

His family holds private services at Davis-Becker Funeral Home in Boardman (Becker Funeral Homes; Vindicator).

Jan 18, 2025

Year-end count

The Vindicator lists him among Youngstown's 20 homicides of 2024. Six cases had led to charges; his is not among them. Capt. Jason Simon says about six more are "solved by exceptional circumstances," without naming them (Vindicator).

Oct 2026

Still open

We found no arrest, charge, reward or public update in news searches through October 2026.

What would settle it

  1. The shooter's car. A make, color or plate from a driver who saw it, or from dash-camera or traffic-camera video.
  2. The rifle. A ballistic match between the casings and a gun recovered in another case.
  3. Why he was followed. Who knew where he would be that afternoon.
Cold Case Checker's assessment

The killing was planned and aimed at him

We put the chance that the shooting was aimed at him by someone who knew him, rather than random road violence, at roughly 85%: police said the casing pattern showed it was targeted and that the shooter followed him. That is our judgment, not a finding. We put the chance of a charge within five years at roughly 30%: rifle casings and a daytime freeway with many witnesses are strong leads, but two years have passed without a public suspect.

Our hypothesis

Editorial reading of public records. A working theory, not a finding, and it names no individual.

Working theory · confidence stated per point

Followed, then fired on

  1. It was planned. Police said the shooter followed the SUV and that the casings showed a targeted attack.
  2. The weapon was a rifle. Firing many rifle rounds from a moving car suggests the shooter came prepared.
  3. The place was risky for the shooter. A daytime rush-hour freeway is full of witnesses, which suggests the shooter acted when the chance came, not at a chosen spot.
  4. The answer is probably in his recent contacts. Someone knew where he would be driving.
  5. Drivers that afternoon are the best hope. Dash-camera footage or a remembered car could name the vehicle.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
