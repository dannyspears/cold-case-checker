Jason Wynn case: a 22-year-old shot behind a church on Forest Avenue, and a father still waiting to learn who pulled the trigger.

On March 1, 2006, Jason T. Wynn, 22, was shot at 521 Forest Avenue in Cincinnati's Avondale neighborhood. His father says he was shot behind a church on Forest Avenue, stumbled out to the street, collapsed and died there. No one has been charged.

Cold Case Checker's conclusion · Oct 2026

Case file at a glance

His father, Terry Jones, has held a vigil for him almost every year since. In 2024 it was at his grave in Vine Street Cemetery. Jones says his son was getting his life together, was trying to be a rapper and had signed with a record label. He left three children; the youngest was born after he died. Jones learned of the killing from television, when he saw a body under a sheet on Forest Avenue.

What is known

  • A cousin said in 2024: "If you know things and you say nothing then you're a part of the problem."

What would settle it

  1. A witness from that day. Someone on Forest Avenue who saw who was behind the church, or who ran.
  2. The people with "ideas." What they know, told to Crime Stoppers anonymously.
  3. Ballistics. Whether casings from the scene match a gun in the national ballistics network (NIBIN).
Cold Case Checker's assessment

A targeted shooting by someone he knew or had a dispute with

We put the chance that he knew the person who shot him at roughly 60%: it happened in a sheltered spot behind a building, nothing suggests a robbery, and his father says people in the community have ideas. That is our judgment, not a finding. We put the chance of a charge within five years at roughly 7%: the case has no public suspect description, though the city's 2024 cold case task force gives it a chance of fresh review.

Our hypothesis

Editorial reading of public records. A working theory, not a finding, and it names no individual.

Working theory · confidence stated per point

A targeted shooting in a sheltered spot

  1. Behind a church, not on the street. He was shot behind a building, out of view, and then made it to the street. That suggests he went there with someone or was met there.
  2. No sign of robbery. No report mentions anything taken; none says either way.
  3. People talked. His father says "some people have ideas." In a neighborhood case, names often circulate early but no one will go on record.
  4. Time changes loyalties. It has been many years. People who were afraid or loyal in 2006 may now be willing to speak, as his father notes happened in other families' cases.
  5. A fresh look is possible. Cincinnati's 2024 cold case task force and modern ballistics checks could reopen a file that has been quiet since 2008 or 2009.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
