The Camrin Starr case: a Friday-night shooting in College Hill, and two men dressed in black.

At about 8:20 p.m. on Friday, May 15, 2015, Cincinnati District Five officers responded to a shooting near Marlowe and Hamilton avenues in College Hill. They found Camrin William De'ron Starr, 30, with gunshot wounds. He died at University of Cincinnati Medical Center. Police said they were looking for two men. No one has been charged.

Cold Case Checker's assessment

A targeted shooting by two men who came to the complex

We put the chance that the shooting was planned and targeted at roughly 60%, because two men dressed alike came together. We put the chance of a charge within five years at roughly 12%: there are two people who could talk, the case had a reward push in 2018, and the city's cold case unit has solved a similar 2015 College Hill case with a witness.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
