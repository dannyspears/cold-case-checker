Kevin Brame case: an off-duty Dayton officer shot in the back after dropping off his sons.

On the evening of Nov 1, 1999, Kevin Brame, 31, a Dayton police officer and father of two, was shot and killed outside his estranged wife's home on Cherry Drive in Dayton after he returned their sons. A neighbor called police at 8:54 p.m. to report gunshots. No one has been charged.

Case file at a glance

Brame was off duty and was hit by a shotgun blast to the back, according to Dayton Daily News and national coverage. Dayton police created a special unit for the case in 2004. The Ohio Attorney General lists it as unsolved homicide 211. A reward of up to $130,000 was reported in 2024.

Cold Case Checker's assessment

A planned ambush by someone who knew he would return the boys that night

We put the chance the shooting was planned at roughly 80%, because he was shot from behind in a place he was expected to be. We put the chance of an arrest within five years at roughly 8%, helped by the reward.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
