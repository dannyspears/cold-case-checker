The Micah Wise case: a 23-year-old found dead on Irving Place before dawn, and a mother hoping a deck of cards will make someone talk.

Shortly before 2:30 a.m. on Friday, January 23, 2009, Cincinnati police responded to a shooting in the 3500 block of Irving Place and found Micah Wise, 23, dead. The Ohio Attorney General gives the address as 3571 Irving Place. No one has been charged.

Cold Case Checker's conclusion · Oct 2026

Case file at a glance

His face is on the cold-case playing cards that the nonprofit UCanSpeakForMe has supplied to the Hamilton County Justice Center since 2014, in the hope that inmates will talk over a game. When the sheriff pulled the cards in October 2021 over a lock-tampering concern, his mother, Joy Marshall, said, "It gave me hope that more people would be aware of my son's murder."

Cincinnati Police homicide investigators take the case and ask for tips to the Criminal Investigations Section (513-352-3542) or Crime Stoppers.

2014 onward

Cold-case cards

UCanSpeakForMe's playing cards, printed with the faces of unsolved-homicide victims, are used in the Hamilton County Justice Center. Micah Wise is among them (when he was added is not stated).

Oct 19, 2021

Cards pulled

Sheriff Charmaine McGuffey removes the cards, saying inmates used them to tamper with cell locks. Joy Marshall says she "couldn't believe it" and that the card "gave me hope."

Oct 23, 2021

Rally

Families and UCanSpeakForMe rally outside the Justice Center for the cards' return. The sheriff says she will find a safe way to keep distributing them.

Cold Case Checker's assessment

A late-night shooting by someone he knew or met that night

We put the chance that he knew or had met the shooter at roughly 55%, a general estimate, not a finding. We put the chance that someone who has passed through the Hamilton County jail has heard who did it at roughly 40%; that is why the cards exist. We put the chance of a charge within five years at roughly 6%.

Working theory · confidence stated per point

A late-night encounter, and someone who has talked since

  1. It was 2:30 a.m. Few people are out; those who were matter.
  2. He died at the scene. He could not tell police anything.
  3. Someone called it in. That caller may have seen or heard more.
  4. Killers talk. Seventeen years on, the shooter may have told someone; the jail cards are aimed at exactly that person.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
