The Ernestine Hurt case: a night-shift worker, a bus stop, and a purse that was not taken.

On June 24, 1982, Ernestine Hurt, 45, a Price Hill woman who cleaned offices at night, was attacked at a bus stop at 6th and Main Streets in downtown Cincinnati. She was stabbed in the chest and died. Her purse was reportedly not taken. No one has been charged.

Case file at a glance

The Hurt case is a detail-poor homicide that her family keeps alive. The central puzzle is motive: police reportedly favored a purse snatching, but the family says her purse, with her bus fare, was left behind.

What would settle it

  1. Confirm what evidence exists. Ask Cincinnati Police for an inventory of 1982 evidence.
  2. Review the 1982 witness statement. A new interview approach could extract more.
  3. Check the White Castle lead with employees from that night, if any can be found.
Cold Case Checker's assessment

A targeted attack is more likely than a robbery

The purse was not taken, per her family, and the attacker ran up to her alone at a bus stop. We put the chance that the attack was targeted at roughly 55%. We put the chance that the case is resolved within five years at 3%.

A quick attack on a woman walking alone, with a motive that is still unknown

  1. He chose the moment. She was alone for the first time, which suggests someone who knew her routine or got lucky.
  2. The motive is unclear. Robbery is undercut by the untouched purse.
  3. The evidence is gone. If police are right that nothing remains, only witnesses and records remain.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
