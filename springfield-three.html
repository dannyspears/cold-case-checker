The Springfield Three: three women, an unlocked door, and thirty-four years of silence.

On the night of June 6–7, 1992, two newly graduated high school friends and one of their mothers vanished from a Springfield, Missouri home. Their cars, purses, and cash were left behind. They have never been found.

Case file at a glance

This is a disappearance, not a recovered-body case, so everything depends on the first hours: who went through the house, what was on the answering machine, and what a broken porch light means. Most of that evidence was compromised before police arrived.

What happened

Times come from news and police accounts. Details of the house come from the first people to enter it.

June 6, 1992 · afternoon

Graduation

Suzanne "Suzie" Streeter, 19, and Stacy McCall, 18, graduate from Kickapoo High School. Suzie's mother, Sherrill Levitt, 47, spends the evening at home working on varnishing an armoire. She is last heard from at about 11:15 p.m.

June 7, 1992 · about 2:00 a.m.

The last sighting

The two friends are last seen leaving a party. They planned to go to a water park in Branson the next morning.

June 7 · morning

The discovery

A friend, Janelle Kirby, and her boyfriend find the front door unlocked at 1717 E. Delmar Street. All three women's cars are outside. Purses, personal items, and about $900 in cash are inside. The only sign of disturbance is a shattered porch light globe.

June 7 · hours later

The house is disturbed

Ten to twenty people go through and tidy the house before police secure it. A message on the answering machine is erased after one playback; police believe it may have been a clue.

1992 – 1997

Tips and searches

Police receive more than 5,000 tips. In 1997 the women are declared legally dead, though their cases are still listed as missing. A memorial bench is dedicated that year.

1997

Robert Cox

Robert Craig Cox, a convicted kidnapper and robber, claims the women were murdered and their bodies will never be found. Investigators are unsure whether he was involved or seeking attention. His 1992 alibi, supported by a girlfriend and his parents, is later undercut when the girlfriend recants.

2003 and 2007

Searches

Investigators dig at farmland near Cassville in 2003 with inconclusive results. A 2007 tip that the women are buried under a hospital parking garage leads to a ground-penetrating radar scan; police say the tip lacked credibility and the garage was built after the disappearances.

June 2026

34 years

Local TV revisits the case. Police still call it an active investigation.

Latest lead: Robert Cox

No suspect has been charged. Cox remains the most prominent name, and nothing new has been published about him in 2026.

Not an accusation. Robert Cox has not been charged in this case. The claim that he is involved is unproven, and investigators have said they were unsure whether he was seeking attention.

Why he is the leading name

  • He told someone in 1997 that the women had been murdered and their bodies would never be found.
  • He was a convicted kidnapper and robber, which fit the pattern police were looking for.
  • His alibi weakened when the girlfriend who supported it later said Cox had asked her to lie.

Our hypothesis

Editorial reading of public records. A working theory, not a finding, and it names no individual.

Working theory · confidence stated per point

Someone entered while the women were home, and the first hours were lost

  1. There was no robbery. Purses, cash, and cars were left. The motive was likely not theft.
  2. The victims likely knew or trusted the person, or were taken by surprise. There were no signs of a struggle beyond the porch light.
  3. The answering-machine message mattered. Someone erased it after one playback. Finding out who and why is the most concrete open question.
  4. The early crowd costs us most. Ten to twenty people moved through the house, and trace evidence is not recoverable.
Abduction, not voluntary departure Victims knew the offender Cox involved Remains located in five years
