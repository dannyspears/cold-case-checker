Tollette “Tonka” George case: a college football star home a month after graduation, shot near his house in Algiers.

Shortly before 10 p.m. on Friday, June 24, 2016, police found Tollette "Tonka" George on the ground near Lauradale Drive and Vespasian Street in Algiers, near where he lived. He had been shot multiple times and died at the scene. A month earlier he had graduated from Alcorn State University, where he played five seasons at receiver. No one has been charged.

Case file at a glance

His killing was one of three in four days in Algiers, which led NOPD to add patrols. The homicide commander said that weekend's victims had been targeted and that each attack grew out of a disagreement, but police did not say what led to George's shooting or whether the cases were linked.

What happened

Details come from Crimestoppers of Greater New Orleans, FOX8 (June 27, 2016), the SWAC (June 25, 2016), WLBT (June 25, 2016) and WGNO (June 25, 2026).

High school

Edna Karr

A standout at Edna Karr High School in Algiers, where he wore No. 5. WGNO calls him a star quarterback there (WGNO; Crimestoppers).

2011 to 2015

Alcorn State

Five seasons as a receiver for the Braves. He led the team in receptions in 2013 with 52 for 637 yards and three touchdowns, helped win back-to-back SWAC championships, and scored the game-tying touchdown in the inaugural Air Force Reserve Celebration Bowl in December 2015 (SWAC; WLBT).

May 2016

Graduation

He graduates from Alcorn State and comes home to New Orleans, hoping to coach football (WLBT; FOX8).

June 24, 2016 · about 10 p.m.

The shooting

Police find him lying on the ground near Lauradale Drive and Vespasian Street. He has been shot multiple times and is pronounced dead at the scene (Crimestoppers; FOX8).

June 24-27, 2016

Three killings in four days

NOPD increases patrols in Algiers after five shootings and three murders. Homicide Commander Doug Eckert says investigators are checking for links, that the victims were targeted and not strangers, and that each attack escalated from a disagreement (FOX8).

June 2016

Mourning

The SWAC and Alcorn State mourn him; teammates post tributes (SWAC; WLBT).

Later

Honors

Edna Karr chooses a player each year to wear his No. 5, and Alcorn State announces a campaign to raise $50,000 for an endowed scholarship in his name (WGNO).

Until June 2024

Reward

Crimestoppers offers up to $2,500; the offer expired June 25, 2024.

June 25, 2026

Ten years

WGNO's Wheel of Justice: "Ten years later, the murder remains unsolved." NOPD is still waiting for the tip that solves it.

Cold Case Checker's assessment

A targeted shooting tied to a local dispute

We put the chance that he was targeted, rather than hit at random, at roughly 60%, based on the police description of that weekend's attacks. Police did not apply it to George by name, so this is our judgment, not a finding. We put the chance of a charge within five years at roughly 8%.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
