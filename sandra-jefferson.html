Sandra Peters Jefferson case: a retired court clerk beaten in her own home on Mother's Day weekend, with no sign of forced entry.

In the early hours of Mother's Day weekend 2012, Sandra "Sandy" Peters Jefferson, 58, was found badly hurt in a bedroom of her Broadmoor home in New Orleans. Police first said she had been shot. She died at about 1:30 a.m. on Saturday, May 12, and the coroner ruled the cause blunt force trauma to the head. Police found several weapons in the house but could not tie any of them to the attack, and saw no sign of forced entry. Fourteen years later, no one has been charged.

What happened

Details come from FOX8 (May 11 and 12, 2012), the Associated Press via WWNO (May 13, 2012), Crimestoppers of Greater New Orleans and WGNO (May 12, 2026). Early reports give slightly different times; we show each.

Before 2012

Who she was

She had recently retired after about ten years as a minutes clerk at Orleans Parish Civil District Court, in Judge Kern Reese's section. She led early-morning workouts at a Broadmoor fitness center her family owned, and neighbors knew her for her garden (FOX8).

May 11, 2012 · about 3:30 a.m.

Found badly hurt

Her husband calls police and says he has just come home and found her on a bedroom floor. She is rushed from the Broadmoor home to Interim LSU Public Hospital (FOX8; WWNO/AP). WGNO's 2026 report puts the call in the early hours of May 12.

May 11, 2012

The scene

Police first report a gunshot wound; Superintendent Ronal Serpas later says he cannot confirm how she was hurt. Weapons are removed from the house, and her husband's Hummer is towed. Serpas says her husband and others have been interviewed (FOX8; WWNO/AP).

May 12, 2012 · about 1:30 a.m.

She dies

The Orleans Parish coroner's office says she died of blunt force trauma to the head (FOX8; WWNO/AP).

After May 2012

No match, no suspect

Police say none of the weapons found in the house could be tied to the attack and that there was no sign of forced entry. No suspect is named (WGNO, 2026).

Undated

Crimestoppers

Crimestoppers lists her case with a reward of up to $2,500. It says her skull was fractured in two places and that gun residue was found at the scene.

May 12, 2026

Cold Case Checker's assessment

An attack by someone who could enter the home

We put the chance that the attacker was someone she knew or let in, rather than a stranger breaking in, at roughly 65%, because police saw no forced entry. That is a reading of one fact and points to no individual. We put the chance of a charge within five years at roughly 8%.

An attack inside the home by someone who could get in

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
