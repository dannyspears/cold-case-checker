The Rocky River Reservation murders: two friends shot on a park bench in daylight, and a $100,000 reward no one has claimed.

On June 4, 2019, longtime friends Kate Brown, 33, and Carnell Sledge, 40, met at a lot north of the Lorain Road bridge in the Rocky River Reservation in Fairview Park, Ohio, and sat on a bench by the river. They were shot in the head between about 5:08 and 5:15 p.m. Two kayakers found them. No one has been charged.

What happened

Details come from the FBI's page, News 5 Cleveland, and a Fox 8 report of May 30, 2025.

June 4, 2019 · about 5:00 p.m.

The meeting

Kate Brown of Fairview Park and Carnell Sledge of North Olmsted, friends for more than ten years and not romantically involved, arrive in separate cars at a pull-off lot north of the Lorain Road bridge.

June 4, 2019 · 5:08 to 5:15 p.m.

The shooting

They sit on a bench by the river and are shot in the head. Sledge is found in the grass and Brown in the water, off Valley Parkway.

June 4, 2019 · 5:18 to 5:30 p.m.

Discovery

Two kayakers see the victims and call 911. Police find the bodies about 5:30 p.m.

2019 to 2020

Rewards grow

The FBI raises its reward from $20,000 to $25,000. Crime Stoppers adds up to $5,000. The family adds up to $70,000, bringing the combined total to $100,000.

May 30, 2025

A possible link

Fox 8 reports Metroparks police are investigating whether a man who died by suicide after shooting his neighbor in an unrelated case weeks earlier has any connection. The chief says there were similarities that warranted an in-depth look. No arrest.

May 2026 (reported)

Hundreds of tips

A search summary says an FBI agent said police have received hundreds of tips and the reward is still $100,000. -Maybe

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
