The William Goebel assassination: a governor shot on the Capitol lawn, and no one ever proved who fired.

On Jan 30, 1900, William Goebel, a candidate disputing a bitterly contested Kentucky governor's election, was shot in Frankfort as he walked to the Old State Capitol. He was sworn in as governor the next day and died on Feb 3. Sixteen people were indicted, three were convicted, and every one of them was eventually pardoned.

Case file at a glance

This is the oldest case on the site and the only assassination of a sitting US governor. Prosecutors said a political conspiracy led by the outgoing Republican administration planned it. The shooter was never established, the main witness changed his account, and the convicted men were pardoned. The case is effectively unsolved.

What happened

Details come from the Wikipedia articles on Goebel and on one of the convicted men, and a LEX18 history piece. We did not read court records or newspapers from 1900.

Jan 30, 1900 · morning

The shooting

Goebel walks to the Old State Capitol in Frankfort with two bodyguards. Shots are fired from the nearby State Building. He is struck in the chest. He had been warned of a rumored plot.

Jan 31, 1900

Sworn in

Goebel is sworn in as governor the day after the shooting, while dying.

Feb 3, 1900

He dies

William Goebel dies. Acting governor Taylor had called out the militia and ordered the legislature into special session in London, Kentucky.

1900 onward

Indictments

Sixteen people are indicted, including Taylor. Three accept immunity for testimony and five go to trial. Two are acquitted.

After 1900

Convictions overturned and retried

The first convictions of the secretary of state and an alleged shooter are overturned on appeal. Each is tried again and convicted again.

About 1902 (year inferred)

A turned witness

A third man, sentenced to life, turns state's evidence after about two years and testifies against the others.

1908 to 1919

Pardons

Governor Augustus Willson pardons the secretary of state and the alleged shooter in 1908 and the former acting governor in 1909. Governor James D. Black pardons the turned witness in 1919.

Suspects

Several people were charged in 1900. All were later acquitted or pardoned.

All of these people are long dead and none holds a surviving conviction. Each was acquitted, pardoned, or never tried. We list what the record says, not guilt.

Convicted twice · pardoned 1908

Caleb Powers, secretary of state

Why named: Prosecutors called him the mastermind. He was convicted of complicity, retried three times, and pardoned.

Against: Republican appeal courts overturned his first conviction. He was pardoned by Governor Willson in 1908 and died in 1932.

No free photo found
Convicted · pardoned 1908

Jim Howard, alleged shooter

Why named: He was accused of being the actual shooter and was convicted three times.

Against: His convictions were overturned on appeal before later ones. He was pardoned by Governor Willson in 1908.

No free photo found
Convicted · pardoned 1919

Henry Youtsey

Why named: He was sentenced to life, then turned state's evidence and testified against Powers and Howard.

Against: The defense pointed to contradictions in his account. Paroled in 1916, pardoned in 1919, died in 1942.

Indicted · never tried

William S. Taylor, acting governor

Why named: He was indicted and fled to Indianapolis.

Against: Indiana refused extradition. Pardoned in 1909. Died in 1928.

What is known

  • Wikipedia says the identity of the assassin remains unknown and that most historians agree on that.
  • The state's main witness gave an account the defense said was contradictory.
  • The convicted men were pardoned after their convictions were questioned.

What would settle it

  1. Reexamine the physical evidence held by the Kentucky Historical Society or Kentucky Department for Libraries and Archives, if it survives.
  2. Review the trial transcripts for each defendant against each other.
  3. Look for new private papers from the participants.
Cold Case Checker's assessment

A planned political killing with a shooter who was shielded

We put the chance that the shooting was planned by a group linked to the outgoing administration at roughly 60%, because prosecutors argued it and the contemporary evidence points that way. We put the chance of the shooter ever being identified at roughly 2%, because the physical evidence is more than 125 years old.

Our hypothesis

Editorial reading of public records. A working theory, not a finding, and it names no individual.

Working theory · confidence stated per point

A coordinated plot around a disputed election, with the shooter never proven

  1. The timing was political. He was shot the day before taking office in a contested election.
  2. The shots came from a government building. They were fired from the State Building next to the Old State Capitol.
  3. The prosecution's case relied on a turned witness. That weakened every verdict.
  4. The outcome was political too. The convicted were pardoned by a governor of the other side.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
