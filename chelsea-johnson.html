Chelsea Johnson case: a 15-year-old found by a creek, a man convicted of other crimes against her, and no murder charge.

On April 15, 2012, Chelsea Johnson, 15, a sophomore at Options Academy in Fairfield, Ohio, disappeared. A family friend found her body the next day near a creek close to the Creekside Village of Fairfield Apartments. She had been stabbed. No one has been charged with her death.

Case file at a glance

George Donald Davis II, whom Fairfield police have described as a person of interest, was indicted in 2012 on drug-trafficking and sex-related charges involving Chelsea, but he has never been charged in her death. A grand jury once declined to indict a suspect. Her family says police work was botched. Fairfield's police chief says the case is still a very active investigation.

What happened

Details come from the Ohio Attorney General (case 3263) and a June 2023 FOX19 report. Reports differ on some details of an earlier arrest.

April 15, 2012

She disappears

Chelsea Johnson, 15, leaves her apartment in Fairfield. She is seen with a neighbor at a convenience store around noon and in the same area later that afternoon, per FOX19.

April 16, 2012

Found

A family friend finds her body near a creek by the Creekside Village of Fairfield Apartments. She had been stabbed. She had been reported missing about nine hours earlier.

June 2012

George Davis II charged with other crimes

George Donald Davis II, 24, a family acquaintance who used to live in Fairfield, is held in the Butler County Jail on $250,000 bond on charges of providing drugs to Chelsea in exchange for sex. A detective testifies that Davis sent text messages offering her muscle relaxers or Valium, then heroin, in exchange for sex on the day she disappeared. Fairfield police later describe him as a person of interest in her death. He is indicted on drug trafficking, importuning and compelling prostitution charges and imprisoned. He is never charged in her death.

2014 to 2016

Unclear steps

WCPO reported an arrest in 2014 and a grand jury did not indict. Other reports say no one was charged. In 2016 Butler County investigators announced a person of interest without saying who; FOX19 says it has never been made clear whether that was Davis or someone else.

Sept 2020

Cold Case Unit

The Ohio Attorney General's new Cold Case Unit adds the case.

June 2023

Family speaks

Her family says police work was botched. Fairfield's police chief says it is still a very active investigation.

Evidence against, or missing

  • No charge in her death in more than fourteen years, despite the indictment on related crimes.
  • No physical evidence linking him to the scene has been made public.
  • The 2016 person of interest has never been identified, so investigators may be looking at someone else.
  • Reports conflict on his sentence for the 2012 charges (about five and a half years in one summary, 15 years in another).

What would settle it

  1. DNA testing of evidence from the creek and her clothing, compared with all persons of interest.
  2. Phone location records for April 15, 2012, compared with the texts.
  3. Identify the 2016 person of interest and whether that person is Davis.
  4. Ohio Attorney General Cold Case Unit review, which began in 2020.

Cold Case Checker's assessment

A killing by someone in her circle that day

We put the chance that she knew her killer or someone near him at roughly 75%. We do not put a number on any named person: the public record shows contact, not a link to the killing. We put the chance of a charge in her death within five years at roughly 12%.

Our hypothesis

Editorial reading of public records. A working theory, not a finding, and it names no individual.

Working theory · confidence stated per point

A killing by someone in the circle of the man who sought her out that day

  1. An adult sought her out that day. A detective testified that the man police have described as a person of interest (see the latest-lead section) texted her offering drugs for sex on the day she disappeared. That shows contact and motive to meet her, not that he killed her.
  2. She was missing for hours. Before she was found.
  3. She was killed near home. Within miles.
  4. The gap is the physical link. No weapon, DNA match or location record tying that man, or anyone else, to the creek has been made public. He was charged only with other crimes, and the 2016 person of interest may be someone else.

Missing links

Questions raised by gaps in the public record.

1. The weapon

The knife and where it went.

What happened and why no indictment.

4. The 2016 person of interest

Who and why. FOX19 says it has never been made clear whether this is the man police described as a person of interest in 2012, or someone else. Settling that would tell us whether investigators have one lead or two.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
