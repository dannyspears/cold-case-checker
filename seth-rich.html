Seth Rich case: a 27-year-old was shot twice in the back a block from home at 4 a.m. Ten years on, the killers have not been found, and the theories have drowned out the facts.

Seth Rich, 27, a data director for the Democratic National Committee, was walking home in the Bloomingdale neighborhood of Washington, D.C., early on Sunday, July 10, 2016. At about 4:20 a.m. he was shot twice in the back in the 2100 block of Flagler Place NW, about a block from his home. A gunfire sensor alerted police, who found him conscious within a minute. He died at a hospital more than an hour and a half later. Police believe he was the victim of an attempted robbery; nothing was taken. No one has been arrested. Online theories tying his death to leaked DNC emails have been rejected by police and contradicted by the special counsel's findings.

Cold Case Checker's assessment

A street robbery that went wrong

We put the chance that this was a street robbery by strangers, as police say, at roughly 85%: the area had a rise in gun robberies, the attack was at 4 a.m. on a residential street, and his mother said they did not finish robbing him. That is our judgment, not a finding. We put the chance of an arrest within five years at roughly 10%: ten years have passed with no public suspect.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
