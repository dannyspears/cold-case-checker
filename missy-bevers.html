Case file · Terri "Missy" Bevers Case
Updated October 15, 2026Status: open, unsolved
STATUSMidlothian police say the case is active, not cold, and that no one has been completely ruled out. More than 3,000 tips have come in. A reward of up to $150,000 has been reported. No arrest.

Terri "Missy" Bevers case: a fitness class moved indoors for the rain, and a figure in police gear already inside the church.

Before dawn on Monday, April 18, 2016, Terri "Missy" Bevers, 45, a mother of three and a Camp Gladiator fitness instructor, arrived at Creekside Church of Christ in Midlothian, Texas, to set up a 5 a.m. class she had moved indoors because of rain. Security cameras had already recorded a person in full police-style tactical gear walking the halls and breaking into rooms. A student arriving for class found her dead. No one has been charged.

Case file at a glance

The video made the case famous: a helmeted figure in a vest marked POLICE, carrying tools, smashing glass and trying doors. Police still cannot say whether the person is a man or a woman, and an FBI-requested gait study could not tell either. Nothing was taken, not even her wedding ring. Ten years on, police say no one has been completely ruled out.

What happened

Details come from CBS Texas, KWTX and Fox 4 (April 2026), CBS Texas (2016 and 2021), NBC DFW (2016 and 2024), and a Wikipedia summary of 2016 coverage and court warrants. Times differ slightly between reports.

Aug 9, 1970

Born

Terri Leann Strickland is born in Graham, Texas, per Wikipedia's summary of her obituary. She later marries and has three daughters, and the family lives in Red Oak.

Apr 17, 2016 · evening

Class moved indoors

She tells students that, because of rain, Monday's 5 a.m. Camp Gladiator class will be held inside the church. The class time and place had been advertised online, CBS Texas noted.

Apr 18, 2016 · before 4 a.m.

Someone already inside

Church cameras record a person in full tactical gear (helmet, heavy vest, gloves) walking the hallways, breaking glass, trying doors and breaking into rooms with a pry bar. The church's exterior cameras are not working that day.

About 4 a.m.

She arrives

Bevers arrives to set up the class. Wikipedia's summary of the footage gives 4:18 a.m.; Fox 4 says around 4 a.m. Police have not released video of where she and the suspect met.

Shortly after 5 a.m.

Found

A student arriving for class finds her. She has puncture wounds to the head and chest. A warrant later says the wounds were consistent with tools the suspect carried; a hammer was found near her, per Wikipedia's summary.

Apr 20, 2016

Not a routine burglary

Police confirm the exterior cameras were off. They say the suspect could be a man or a woman. It is Midlothian's first murder in seven years. Oak Farms Dairy offers a $10,000 reward; the ATF helps search with a dog.

Apr 22 to June 6, 2016

A dead end

A relative's shirt with blood on it, taken to a dry cleaner after the killing, is seized; lab tests show the blood is not human. The family had said it came from a dog injured in a fight. Police call it a dead end.

May 2016

A car in a nearby lot

Police release an image of a 2010 to 2012 Nissan Altima seen circling the SWFA Outdoors parking lot early that morning. Full footage follows in December 2016. Police later say they want to speak with its driver but do not believe that person was involved.

Oct 27, 2016

Reward rises

Two anonymous donors add $15,000 each, raising the reward to $50,000 (NBC DFW).

Apr 19, 2021

The gait study

CBS Texas reports that the FBI asked a forensic podiatrist to study the suspect's walk. He says the gear changes how someone walks, the out-toed gait is common, and gait cannot show whether the killer is a man or a woman.

April 2021

Reward reaches $150,000

The reward is reported at $150,000 (summarized by Wikipedia from KRLD/Audacy).

Feb 2024

Her husband speaks

In a public interview, her husband says he was on a fishing trip in Mississippi when police called, that investigators "had to take a look" at him, and that he believes the killing was random.

April 2026

Ten years

Midlothian police say the case is not cold: more than 3,000 tips over the decade and more than 100 in the past year, newer forensic techniques, and no one completely ruled out. Friends hold a tip drive at Kimmel Park.

Cold Case Checker's assessment

A planned intrusion by someone who expected to find her inside

We put the chance that the attacker came for her, rather than interrupting a burglary, at roughly 60%: nothing was taken, the class time was public, and it had moved indoors only the night before. We put the chance of a charge within five years at roughly 12%; police still review new tips and forensic methods, but the suspect's face and sex remain unknown.

Our hypothesis

Editorial reading of public records. A working theory, not a finding, and it names no individual.

Working theory · confidence stated per point

Someone who knew she would be there

  1. The class was public, and it moved. The time and place were advertised online, and she told students the night before that it would be inside the church.
  2. The intruder was already inside. The suspect was recorded in the building before she arrived, around 4 a.m. on a Monday.
  3. It does not look like a burglary. Doors and glass were broken, but nothing was taken: not her ring, purse or iPad.
  4. The disguise was deliberate. Police-style gear hides face, build and sex, and could make a victim hesitate.
  5. The answer may be in who saw the change of venue. Students, social media followers, or anyone watching the church.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
