The Abrams and Francis murders: two people shot in the back of the head, and seven money orders that never turned up.

Before dawn on March 10, 1985, Nancy Abrams, 33, working the overnight shift at the King Kwik gas station on Breiel Boulevard in Middletown, was shot twice in the back of the head. John Francis, 31, a customer, was shot once and left in a back room. No one has been convicted.

Case file at a glance

Police pursued robbery, personal-motive and serial-killer theories, exhumed Abrams, hypnotized witnesses, and made a sketch of a motorcycle rider. A 2004 detective developed a suspect and took the case to a grand jury, which returned no indictment. The prosecutor later called the evidence very circumstantial.

What happened

Details come from a Nov 2018 Dayton Daily News story and a March 2021 FOX19 report. The store is spelled King Kwik in one and King Kwick in others.

March 10, 1985 · early morning

Last sighting

A newspaper delivery woman sees Nancy Abrams working at the King Kwik on Breiel Boulevard during her morning drop-off.

About 4:30 a.m.

The motorcycle rider

The delivery woman sees a man she describes as a motorcycle rider outside the store. This leads to a composite sketch.

March 10, 1985

The discovery

Abrams is found dead near a storage room behind the counter, shot twice in the back of the head. John Francis, 31, of Fernwood Street, is found dead in a back room near the freezer, shot once in the back of the head. His brown AMC Concord is parked near the front. Police wake Abrams's husband at about 7:30 a.m.

1985 onward

Investigation

Police pursue robbery, personal-motive and serial-killer theories. They exhume Abrams and try hypnosis with people who were near the store.

2004

Grand jury

A third detective develops a suspect and presents the case to a grand jury under then-prosecutor Robin Piper. No indictment is returned. Fingerprints on glass soda bottles are tested the same year with no result.

Nov 12, 2018

Fresh look

A Middletown detective reviews the file and a victims advocate appeals for tips.

March 2021

No update

FOX19 reports Middletown police have shared no new updates.

What would settle it

  2. Check the money orders against records to see if any were cashed.
  3. Review the grand jury file with current standards.
Cold Case Checker's assessment

A robbery or personal killing by someone who walked in and shot both quickly

We put the chance that the killer came to rob the store at roughly 45%, because money orders were taken. We put the chance that the crime was personal toward Abrams at roughly 35%. We put the chance of a solution within five years at roughly 8%.

Our hypothesis

Editorial reading of public records. A working theory, not a finding, and it names no individual.

Working theory · confidence stated per point

A fast double killing at a store with little cash

  1. Both were shot execution style. That suggests control and speed.
  2. The money orders were taken. A robbery motive fits, but the cash was not the only prize.
  3. It happened before dawn. The store was one of few open overnight.
  4. The evidence is old. Without DNA, the case rests on witnesses and records.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
