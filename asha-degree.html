Case file · Asha Degree Case
Updated October 20, 2026Status: missing, active
STATUSAsha has never been found. Investigators wrote in 2024 search warrants that they believe she was killed and her body concealed; her parents believe she is alive. Members of one Cleveland County family are named in warrants, and two are called suspects. No one has been charged. The combined reward is $100,000.

Asha Degree case: a 9-year-old who walked out into a stormy night, and a backpack found buried 18 months later.

Sometime after 2:30 a.m. on Monday, February 14, 2000, Asha Jaquilla Degree, 9, left her family's home on Oakcrest Drive outside Shelby, North Carolina, with a bookbag she had packed. Between about 3:45 and 4:15 a.m., a truck driver and a motorist saw a girl matching her description walking south on Highway 18 in a storm. When the motorist circled back, she ran into the woods. Her parents found her bed empty before 6:30 a.m. She has never been found.

Case file at a glance

In August 2001 her backpack was dug up at a construction site off Highway 18 in Burke County, about 26 miles away, wrapped in two black trash bags. In 2024 and 2025 the Cleveland County Sheriff's Office, the FBI and the SBI searched properties tied to Roy and Connie Dedmon's family after DNA on items from the backpack was linked to one of their daughters and to a man who lived in their care homes. The Dedmons deny any involvement, and no one has been charged.

What happened

Details come from the FBI, WBTV's reporting on the unsealed warrants (2024-2026), WCNC, Spectrum News, NewsNation, Sinclair/WLOS, and Wikipedia's summary of the Shelby Star and Charlotte Observer archives, which we could not open directly.

Aug 5, 1990

Born

Asha Jaquilla Degree is born in Shelby to Harold and Iquilla Degree. Her brother is a year older (Wikipedia, citing the Shelby Star).

Feb 2000

Who she was

A fourth grader at Fallston Elementary and a point guard on a youth basketball team. Her mother described her as cautious and shy (Wikipedia, citing Jet and ABC News).

Feb 12, 2000

A lost game

Her team loses its first game of the season; she fouls out and is upset but seems to recover (Wikipedia, citing the Shelby Star).

Feb 13, 2000 · Sunday

Church and bed

The family attends church; a cousin later recalled Asha ushering at Macedonia Missionary Baptist Church. Asha and her brother go to bed around 8 p.m. in their shared room (Wikipedia; WCNC). A nearby car crash knocks out power; it returns about 12:30 a.m. and her father sees both children asleep (Wikipedia).

Feb 14, 2000 · 2:30 a.m.

Last seen at home

Her father checks again and sees both children in bed. Her brother later recalled hearing her bed squeak (FBI; Wikipedia).

Feb 14, 2000 · 3:45 to 4:15 a.m.

On Highway 18

A truck driver and a motorist see a girl walking south on Highway 18 near its junction with Highway 180, in a long-sleeved white T-shirt and white pants. The motorist circles three times; the girl runs into the woods (Wikipedia, citing WBTV and ABC News).

Feb 14, 2000 · before 6:30 a.m.

Reported missing

Her mother finds her bed empty; the family reports her missing by 6:30 a.m. Dogs cannot pick up a scent (FBI; Wikipedia).

Feb 15, 2000

The shed

Searchers find candy wrappers, a pencil, a green marker and a hair bow in a shed at a business near where she entered the woods. Her parents identify the pencil, marker and bow as hers (Wikipedia, citing the Shelby Star).

Feb 2000

Search ends

After about 9,000 hours and 300 leads, the active search is called off. Sheriff Dan Crawford: "We have never really had that first good, substantial lead." An SBI agent calls her "not your typical runaway" (Wikipedia, citing the Charlotte Observer and Shelby Star).

Aug 2001

The backpack

Construction workers find her bookbag buried off Highway 18 in Burke County, near Morganton, wrapped in two black trash bags. It has her name and phone number on it. Inside are a Dr. Seuss library book, McElligot's Pool, from her school, and a New Kids on the Block concert T-shirt, neither of which was hers (WCNC; FBI; Wikipedia).

Nov 2004

A false lead

After a jail inmate's tip, deputies dig at an intersection in Lawndale; the bones are from an animal (Wikipedia, citing the Charlotte Observer).

2008 on

Family walks

The Degrees start a scholarship in her name and an annual walk to her billboard on Highway 18 (Wikipedia).

Feb 2015

Review

The FBI, sheriff and SBI re-examine the case; the FBI offers up to $25,000 (Wikipedia, citing an FBI release).

May 25, 2016

A green car

The FBI says she may have gotten into a dark green early-1970s Lincoln Mark IV or Ford Thunderbird with rust around the wheel wells, on Highway 18. Sheriff Alan Norman says the car "was occupied two times on the day of her disappearance" (WBTV).

Sept 2017

FBI team

The FBI's Child Abduction Rapid Deployment team spends ten days in Cleveland County (Wikipedia, citing an FBI release).

Oct 2018

The book and the shirt

The sheriff's office asks the public about the library book and the concert T-shirt, calling them vital clues (Wikipedia; WCNC).

Feb 2020

Twenty years

The FBI confirms the book and shirt were in the bag and were not hers. The National Center for Missing and Exploited Children releases a new age-progressed image (FBI; WCNC).

May 2024

"Significant progress"

The FBI says investigators are making significant progress. Her mother says: "I do not believe she is dead" (WCNC).

Sept 10-11, 2024

Searches

Investigators execute eight warrants on Roy and Connie Dedmon, their daughter AnnaLee Dedmon Ramirez, homes in Shelby and Charlotte, and the North Brook Rest Home in Vale. They seize a green 1964 AMC Rambler, computers, phones, journals and a DNA sample. No human remains are found. The warrants say Asha was a "victim of homicide, with her body concealed" (WBTV).

Sept 13, 2024

The family responds

The Dedmons' attorney, David Teddy, says the family denies any involvement or knowledge, calls the connection "tenuous, at best," and says the evidence will "sadly link" a person "no longer living" (WBTV; WCNC).

Feb 13, 2025

Phones seized

New warrants seize phones from Roy Dedmon and daughters Lizzie Dedmon Foster and Sarah Dedmon Caple, citing probable cause for felony obstruction of justice. The warrants quote family text messages and a man's claim of an admission at a mid-2000s party. Roy and Connie Dedmon are called suspects (WBTV).

Apr 4, 2025

Another search

The FBI, SBI and Cleveland and Lincoln County deputies search buildings along Highway 274 and NC 182, including the old North Brook Elementary School; the owner consented and is not connected (Spectrum News).

May-Sept 2025

Reward

The sheriff raises its reward to $75,000 in May; the governor adds $25,000 in June. Billboards on Highways 18 and 180 advertise $100,000 (WBTV; WCNC).

Feb 14, 2026

Twenty-six years

No one has been arrested or charged. WBTV reports no known connection between the Dedmon and Degree families (WBTV).

Cold Case Checker's assessment

Picked up on Highway 18 and taken by someone local, with others helping conceal it

We put the chance that Asha was picked up on Highway 18 by someone local, rather than staying lost in the woods, at roughly 80%, because her backpack was buried 26 miles away. We put the chance that more than one person helped conceal what happened at roughly 65%, in line with investigators' own reading. We name no individual in that judgment. We put the chance of a charge within five years at roughly 35%, higher than most cases here, because investigators have DNA, a seized car and named suspects.

Picked up on Highway 18

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
