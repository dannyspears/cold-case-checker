Brandon Adams and Christine Marcelin murders: two eighth graders, a young couple, killed three days apart.

On Friday night, April 27, 2012, Brandon Adams, 15, was walking home from a park in the Desire area of New Orleans with his older brother when gunmen opened fire near Press and Higgins. Brandon was shot in the head and died; his brother was wounded. Three days later, on Monday morning, April 30, the body of his girlfriend, Christine Marcelin, 15, was found shot several times in the 5000 block of Alcee Fortier Boulevard in New Orleans East. Both were eighth graders at KIPP Believe College Prep.

Case file at a glance

In 2015 prosecutors charged Brandon's older brother with Christine's murder, saying he blamed her for Brandon's death. In August 2017 a jury acquitted him of murder, kidnapping and conspiracy, convicting him only of obstruction of justice for not turning over his phone. No one has ever been charged with Brandon's killing.

What happened

Details come from FOX8 (May 2012), The Advocate (Aug 2017), Crimestoppers of Greater New Orleans and WGNO (May 2024).

Apr 27, 2012 · Friday night

Brandon is shot

Walking home from a park with his 18-year-old brother near Press and Higgins, in the Desire area, Brandon is shot in the head and dies. His brother is wounded (FOX8: in the back; WGNO: in the leg).

Apr 30, 2012 · about 7 a.m.

Christine is found

A homeless man finds her body in the 5000 block of Alcee Fortier Boulevard in New Orleans East. She has been shot several times (FOX8; Crimestoppers).

May 1, 2012

Vigil at school

Students, parents and teachers gather on the KIPP Believe playground, light candles and walk around the school. Superintendent Ronal Serpas says detectives have "good leads": "Whenever a child is killed, it gives a certain urgency to us all" (FOX8).

2012 to 2015

Reward

Crimestoppers offers a reward for the arrest and indictment of those responsible, up to $5,000, split if a tip clears only one killing. It expired May 1, 2015.

2015

A charge in Christine's death

About three years later, prosecutors charge Brandon's older brother with Christine's murder. The district attorney later says the case had gone cold until two jail inmates came forward during a gang investigation (The Advocate).

Aug 22, 2017

Acquitted

After a week-long trial, a jury finds him not guilty of second-degree murder, conspiracy and second-degree kidnapping. He is convicted of obstruction of justice for not turning over a phone he used that night. Prosecutors said he blamed Christine for his brother's death; the defense attacked the inmates' credibility. There was little physical evidence (The Advocate).

2017

Sentence

He is sentenced to 20 years on the obstruction count (WGNO, 2024).

May 2, 2024

Twelve years

WGNO's Wheel of Justice: both cases remain open and unsolved.

Cold Case Checker's assessment

Two linked killings rooted in a neighborhood conflict

We put the chance that the two killings are connected at roughly 70%: a dating couple killed three days apart. That is our judgment, not a finding, and it names no one. We put the chance of a charge in either case within five years at roughly 8%.

Our hypothesis

Working theory · confidence stated per point

One conflict, two deaths

  1. The timing. A dating couple killed three days apart is unlikely to be chance.
  2. Brandon's case is the key. No one was charged in the first shooting, and the motive for the second may run back to it.
  3. The 2017 theory failed. A jury rejected the idea that Christine was killed in revenge by the man prosecutors charged.
  4. Someone knows. Both were killed in neighborhoods where people talk; Crimestoppers tips are anonymous.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
