Case file · Yasmin Acree Case
Updated October 16, 2026Status: missing, unsolved
STATUSYasmin has never been found, and her death has not been confirmed. Her family believes she is dead. No one has been charged. Chicago police said in January 2020 that the case was still in progress, with few leads; a $10,000 reward was reported.

Yasmin Acree case: a 15-year-old gone from her basement bedroom, cut locks on the gate and door, and her glasses left behind.

On the night of January 15, 2008, Yasmin Acree, 15, a freshman at Austin Polytechnical Academy, went to bed in the basement bedroom of her family's two-flat in the 4800 block of West Congress Parkway on Chicago's West Side. By morning she was gone. Her family found two locks cut on an outside gate and the basement door forced. Police first treated her as a runaway. She has never been found, and no one has been charged.

Oct 25, 1992

Born

Yasmin Rayon Acree is born. She later lived with an aunt by marriage, who adopted her in 2006 (Charley Project).

Jan 15, 2008

Last day

She attends activities at the North Lawndale YMCA, comes home, does laundry and goes to bed in her basement bedroom (Charley Project).

Jan 16, 2008

Gone

She is gone in the morning. Her family reports two gate locks cut and the basement door forced. Police say there is no evidence of a break-in and that she had told friends she planned to run away; a friend later recants (Tribune, via Beachwood Reporter).

Jan 17–18, 2008

The lock

Police inventory a lock the family pointed out. Her adoptive mother files a complaint (Sun-Times, via Beachwood Reporter).

Jan 25, 2008

First reward

A clergy group offers $1,000 (Tribune, via Beachwood Reporter).

Jul 2008

2,000 hours

Police say they have no suspects, have spent more than 2,000 hours on the case, and followed leads to California and Kentucky (Tribune, via Beachwood Reporter).

May 2009

Neighbor arrested

Jimmie Terrell Smith, who had lived in the second-floor apartment of her building, is arrested for kidnapping and raping two 14-year-old girls and two young women in Englewood (DNAinfo).

Sep 2009

Misconduct sustained

Internal Affairs writes that "misconduct on the part of the Department member(s) has been proven." The superintendent says officers made a mistake that did not affect the search (ABC7).

Mar 4, 2011

A search

Police search a South Side home where Smith had lived on and off, collecting four bags of evidence (Tribune, via Beachwood Reporter). Results have not been made public.

Mar 12, 2011

"I know what happened to her"

The Tribune reports that her diary twice mentions him, and that in three jail interviews he said he knew what happened to her without admitting involvement. Detectives had questioned him twice, once for more than 30 hours (via Beachwood Reporter).

Aug 2013

A jail meeting

Smith asks to meet her adoptive mother and her relative the Rev. Ira Acree at Cook County Jail. Her adoptive mother says he claimed Yasmin took her own life at a house in the 2200 block of South Spaulding Avenue and that he disposed of her body. She says she does not believe that account (Austin Weekly News, 2014).

Jan 2014

Family believes she is dead

At a vigil, the family says it believes Yasmin is dead. Police and prosecutors decline to comment (Austin Weekly News).

Feb 2014

Her adoptive mother dies

She dies of natural causes at 57 (Charley Project; Chicago Tribune).

Mar 15, 2016

Guilty plea

Smith pleads guilty in the 2009 kidnappings and rapes (DNAinfo).

Sep 26, 2017

110 years

Smith is sentenced to 110 years in prison for the 2009 kidnappings and rapes (DNAinfo).

Jan 2018

Call to reopen

At a vigil, the Rev. Ira Acree asks prosecutors to reopen the case and points to Smith's "unused confession." A $10,000 reward is reported (Austin Weekly News).

Oct 2019

Murder-for-hire verdict

Smith is found guilty of trying to hire a hitman to kill a judge and a prosecutor (Sun-Times).

Jan 16, 2020

Still in progress

A Chicago police spokesperson says the case is still in progress, with few leads. The $10,000 reward remains (ABC7).

Mar 2023

New age progression

An age progression to 30 is posted (Charley Project).

Named in news reports · not charged

Jimmie Terrell Smith

Why named: He lived in the second-floor apartment of her building after his release on parole in 2005, and her diary twice mentions him. He told the Tribune in 2011, "I know what happened to her," and in a 2013 jail meeting gave her family an account of her death. Police questioned him twice, once for more than 30 hours, and searched a home where he had lived. In 2017 he was sentenced to 110 years for kidnapping and raping four girls and women in 2009.

Against: He has never been charged in her case and has never publicly admitted involvement. The family did not believe his 2013 account, and the Tribune warned his claims could be fabrications. No physical evidence linking him to her has been made public.

Cold Case Checker's assessment

Taken from the home by someone who knew the house

We put the chance that Yasmin was taken from the home, rather than leaving on her own, at roughly 75%, because of the cut locks and the glasses she left behind. That is our judgment, not a finding. We put the chance of a charge within five years at roughly 8%, because no physical evidence has been made public and the case has had few leads since 2020. We do not estimate any named person's guilt.

Our hypothesis

Editorial reading of public records. A working theory, not a finding, and it names no individual.

Working theory · confidence stated per point

Taken by someone who knew the house

  1. The locks were cut from outside. Two gate locks and the basement door point to someone coming in, not a girl slipping out.
  2. She left her glasses. She could not see well without them. A planned runaway would likely have taken them.
  3. Someone knew where she slept. Her bedroom was in the basement, behind the door that was forced.
  4. The first days were lost. Police treated it as a runaway and the lock sat for days before it was dusted; early evidence may be thin.
  5. Someone has talked. At least one person has claimed knowledge. Whoever can confirm or disprove that account is the missing link.

Solved cases

Local cases that moved after decades.

Carol Sue Klaber (Boone County, KY, 1976)

Investigators say a private genealogy investigation helped them identify Thomas Dunaway, who died in 1990, as the man who killed the 16-year-old. He cannot be indicted, so prosecutors sent a letter saying he would have been charged. The lesson: genealogy can name a killer after decades, even when no trial is possible.

Forensic genetic genealogy training

In 2024, local investigators were being trained in the technique, which has already helped solve Cincinnati-area cases, according to FOX19. The lesson: the tool is now local, so cases with usable DNA have a real path forward.

Golden State Killer

Old crime-scene DNA matched relatives in a public database and led to a 2018 arrest. The lesson: a usable sample can still work after decades.
